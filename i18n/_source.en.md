# Sarasvatī — a global open archive for classical canons

**Sarasvatī** (Tibetan: དབྱངས་ཅན་མ། · Sanskrit: सरस्वती) is a global open archive
for the protection and cross-lingual translation of world classical canons.

## Why this exists

Classical texts are humanity's common inheritance. They should not be locked
behind copyright, burnt by war, or lost by broken links. Sarasvatī does three
things:

1. **Translate.** For public-domain source texts, we build AI-assisted machine
   drafts into languages that still lack a translation, and openly invite
   expert human review.
2. **Archive.** Every artifact is mirrored in at least three locations
   (GitHub, local, cloud) with SHA-256 integrity manifests.
3. **Release.** Everything is published under CC BY-SA 4.0. Derivatives must
   remain open.

## Current release

**v0.2.0** (2026-08-28):

- 📜 **Suśruta Saṃhitā · Sūtrasthāna** — all 46 chapters translated from
  Bhishagratna 1907 (public domain English) into Tibetan by a four-way
  parallel AI pipeline. Zero residual Chinese characters. Uncertain items
  are marked ⟨བརྟག⟩ / ⟨བརྟག་དགོས།⟩ for expert review.
- 📊 Structured timeline data — 80 canonical-transmission events across 8
  Buddhist traditions (india, sanskrit, pali, seasia, silkroad, chinese,
  sinosphere, tibetan) as JSONL/CSV.
- 📋 Project plan, style guide, term glossary, and reference materials.

## Roadmap (eight branches)

Sarasvatī's long-term structure follows the world Buddhist canon
transmission timeline: **India · Sanskrit manuscripts · Pāli · Southeast
Asian Theravāda · Silk Road · Chinese canon · Sinosphere · Tibetan**.
Each branch may host multiple translation projects. The current active
project is under the **Tibetan** branch: continuing the remaining 140
chapters of the Suśruta Saṃhitā.

## ⚠️ Disclaimer

All translations here are **machine drafts** pending expert human review.
Do not rely on them as authoritative for medical, ritual, or scholarly
purposes without validation by qualified specialists.
