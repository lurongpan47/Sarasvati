## Which principle applied
- **Satya** — the branch's whole reason to exist is "what the folio actually says vs. what the Pāli says". Printing the *expected* `prasaṃsati` over the *attested* `prasajhati` erased the one data point that verse contributes. That is not a typo; it is the failure mode itself.
- **Asteya** — the corrected text is still 100 % from a PD edition; nothing from Brough 1962 was consulted or incorporated.
- **Sati / anattā** — the erratum table names the errors instead of quietly overwriting them; the superseded manifests and the v0.9-b IPFS snapshot still hold the wrong text, provably dated.
- **Sampajañña** — before regenerating the manifest I re-derived the recipe and diffed it against the existing manifest so that the only changes were the files I touched.

## What I rejected
- **Extending Silk Road by a fifth verse** (the literal option-1 text). Rejected: adding to a file whose existing "attested" column was unverified would compound the debt.
- **Quiet in-place fix, no erratum.** Rejected, same reason as 09-18: anyone who pinned v0.9-b or verified the 967579 manifest deserves to know what changed and why.
- **Marking the corrected column ⟨བརྟག⟩ anyway.** Rejected: the column is now a reproduction of a printed PD edition read from the scan; the residual uncertainty (Senart's readings vs. the folio, Brough's later corrections) is the *reviewer's* job and is stated in the review section, not something a draft-mark can express.
- **Doing Hultzsch / Ashoka in the same push.** Rejected: one thing per push; this took the full cycle to do carefully.
- **Keeping the wrong "B–M reproduce the plates" line** as harmless. Rejected: it would send a reviewer to the wrong book.

## What would have blocked me
- If the scan had been unreadable at the relevant pages (OCR too damaged to settle `prasajhati` vs. `prasaṃṣati`), I would have flagged the discrepancy in the file as "unverified, OCR ambiguous" and not changed the reading. It was legible; Senart's note on the aspirate stroke and B–M's "we should have expected" made it unambiguous.
- If the DLI item had turned out to be a post-1929 reprint with a copyright notice, I would have stopped and looked for the 1921 original. Colophon dates the Preface 1921, Calcutta University Press.
