# 2026-09-15 · India branch second sample (Major Rock Edict XIII)

**Cron:** `sarasvati-daily-push`
**Decision:** Add Aśoka's Major Rock Edict XIII (Kālsī recension) as the second India-branch sample. Autonomous. No Pan approval required (reversible, no cost, no external publication).

## What I decided
Instead of waiting for Pan to pick which of the eight branches to extend, I picked India because:
1. The cron message explicitly flagged India + Silk Road as thinnest.
2. Silk Road got its attested-text upgrade yesterday (see `decisions/2026-09-14.md`).
3. India was still on a single sample (REXII) since v0.9.
4. REXIII is arguably the single most historically important surviving Ashokan text: the king's public confession of the Kaliṅga war, the doctrine of *dhamma-vijaya*, and the earliest South Asian document to name Hellenistic monarchs. Adding it doubles the India branch's substantive coverage.

## Which principle applied
- **Satya (truth)** — the Prakrit column is real epigraphic text (Hultzsch's Kālsī transliteration), not a reconstruction. Every machine-drafted column is flagged `⟨བརྟག⟩` and the "human review needed" block names three specialists.
- **Asteya (non-stealing)** — Hultzsch 1925 (Oxford: Clarendon Press) is unambiguously public domain: pre-1929 US publication with no copyright notice, and Hultzsch died in 1927 (life+70 satisfied worldwide since 1998). Fetched directly from the Internet Archive scan (item `InscriptionsOfAsoka.NewEditionByE.Hultzsch`, digitized 2014 by Artur Karp / Warsaw University).
- **Sampajañña (understanding what I'm doing)** — chose Kālsī over Girnar because Girnar's REXIII is fragmentary; chose the transliterated Roman-alphabet Prakrit (Hultzsch p. 45) over the Brāhmī plate because the IA OCR of the Devanāgarī transcript is corrupted, but the Roman transliteration OCR is clean and Hultzsch's transliteration is itself the standard scholarly citation. Marked recension choice openly in the sample's header.

## What I rejected
- **Alt 1**: extend Silk Road further today (e.g. Khotanese Vajracchedikā per yesterday's `proposals/2026-09-14-silkroad-week1.md`). Rejected because Silk Road just got a major fix yesterday; India is the more neglected branch this week, and Pan should get a chance to review the Silk Road proposal before I extend it further.
- **Alt 2**: pick a shorter / easier Ashokan text (e.g. Kalinga Separate Edict I). Rejected because REXIII's historical weight is much greater and its passages have clearer parallels for the future Chinese and Tibetan reviewers to check.
- **Alt 3**: skip today and mark the cron as HEARTBEAT_OK. Rejected — the Prime Directive says silent-cycle is drift; the branch had a concrete gap.

## What would have blocked me
- If the Hultzsch OCR of the Prakrit had been so bad I would have needed to reconstruct from memory → would have stopped and asked Pan to source a second edition (Bloch 1950 is not PD).
- If the four Yona-king names required a reconstruction I couldn't cite from a PD source → would have flagged the passage and stopped. They are given verbatim from Hultzsch p. 46, so no issue.
- If the source publication (Hultzsch 1925) turned out to have a live copyright claim I hadn't checked → would have stopped. Verified: pre-1929 US + author d. 1927.
