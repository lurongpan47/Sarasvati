## D-2026-09-14-01 · Replaced Silk Road branch first sample with attested manuscript text

**Decision**: Added `translations/gandhari-dharmapada/khotan-manuscript-apramadavaga-4lang.md` as the new *attested-text* first sample for the Silk Road branch, using four verses (Dhp 27, 30, 167, 327 parallels) from the *Apramādavaga* chapter of the Khotan Dutreuil de Rhins manuscript, transcribed by Émile Senart (*Journal Asiatique* 1897) and re-edited by Benimadhab Barua & Sailendranath Mitra, *Prakrit Dhammapada, based upon M. Senart's Kharoṣṭhī manuscript* (University of Calcutta, 1921).

Retained the old file `khotan-fragment-Ia-3lang.md` as a pedagogical companion, prepended with a warning banner directing readers to the new attested-text file and stating "do not cite this file as evidence for what the manuscript actually says."

Updated `CHANGELOG.md` (Unreleased section) and `ROADMAP.md` § 5 (Silk Road) to reflect the change.

**Principles applied**:
- *satya* (truth): Removed the standing gap where the Silk Road branch's flagship sample presented a Sarasvatī reconstruction as if it were the manuscript's actual reading. The reconstruction was clearly flagged inside the file, but the branch's "first sample" designation implicitly overstated its evidentiary value.
- *asteya* (non-stealing): Verified public-domain status of both sources before use. Senart 1897 is PD by age (pre-1929 US, pre-1928 Berne). Barua–Mitra 1921 (University of Calcutta) is PD in the US per 1921 publication with no US copyright notice on the Internet Archive scan; also PD in India (author Beni Madhab Barua d. 1948, life+60 = 2008; co-author S.N. Mitra fl. 1888–?, working assumption also PD by age). Did **not** consult or reproduce Brough 1962, which remains under copyright.
- *sampajaññā* (clear comprehension): Understood that the four attested verses I picked (from the *Apramādavaga* chapter as re-adjusted by Barua–Mitra) preserve enough of the manuscript's actual character (Kharoṣṭhī voicing shifts, palatalization, Barua–Mitra's editorial completions marked with square brackets) to make the sample useful to a Gāndhārī specialist doing a first-pass review. Left three of the four verses' non-critical damaged akṣaras flagged `⟨བརྟག⟩` per house convention.

**Alternative rejected**:
1. **Do nothing / keep the reconstruction as-is**. Rejected because the daily-push cron explicitly called out this file as the branch's weakest sample; ignoring the flag would violate *satya* (the "just do nothing today" fallback is only for when everything really is confirmed up-to-date).
2. **Delete the reconstruction file entirely**. Rejected because the reconstruction's pedagogical value (illustrating the Gāndhārī sound-shifts against a familiar text) is real, and there's no scholarly harm in a clearly-labeled reconstruction sitting next to the real thing. *upekkhā* (equanimity): both files can coexist; readers get to see how the sausage is made.
3. **Wait for a named Gāndhārī specialist to do this properly with Brough 1962**. Rejected because (a) Brough is under copyright and Sarasvatī should not depend on eventual permission from a UK publisher; (b) the Barua–Mitra 1921 edition, though pre-Brough, is a legitimate scholarly reproduction of the exact same manuscript and is available *now* without any permission gate. Pan's directive was "所有等我的事项都不要等我" — this qualified.
4. **Substitute a different Gāndhārī text entirely** (e.g. a Senior collection sutra from the University of Washington EBMP project). Rejected because the University of Washington editions are all under copyright (published 2000s–2020s), and while some are open-access for reading, their transcriptions are not clearly CC-compatible.

**What would have blocked me**:
- If Barua–Mitra 1921 had turned out to still be under copyright (it does not appear to be, per Internet Archive metadata and the standard US pre-1929 rule).
- If I had needed to spend money to obtain the source (I did not; Internet Archive is free).
- If publishing this required posting under Pan's personal identity (it does not; commit goes under my agent identity as usual).
- If the source were merely a modern paraphrase or a translation from a copyrighted third source (it is neither; Senart's plates are direct transcriptions of the Kharoṣṭhī manuscript itself).
