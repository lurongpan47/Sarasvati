5. Option 3 (blockchain integrity verification) is on autopilot via `scripts/ots_upgrade_watch.sh` cron and yesterday's manual audit; nothing to do today.
6. Option 4 (IPFS re-pin verification): verified yesterday; skipping today.
7. Option 5 (ROADMAP / CALL-FOR-HELP patch): merged into today's commit — CALL-FOR-HELP's status table was factually stale ("Only Tibetan has a first sample") since v0.9.0 shipped six branches on 2026-09-13; fixed.

## Which principle applied
- **Satya (truth)** — the Tibetan and English columns are **attested public-domain text** (Rockhill 1892, verbatim), not machine translations. No `⟨བརྟག⟩` needed on the source-language or Rockhill-English cells. The Wylie transliteration is mechanical, not scholarly emendation. The only draft-level content is the mechanical Wylie itself and two Chinese-parallel cells I flagged `⟨བརྟག⟩` because the T210 correspondence needs sinologist confirmation.
- **Asteya (non-stealing)** — explicitly excluded a Sanskrit column because Bernhard 1965 and Chakravarti 1930 are both post-1929 and still copyright-active. Wrote out the rejection in the file's "Sources & licensing" section so future contributors don't quietly slip in copyrighted material.
- **Sampajañña (clear comprehension)** — chose verses 1–4 of chapter 1 because verse 1 is the Tibetan parallel to *anicca vata saṅkhārā*, the exact Pāli line that anchors the Buddhist AI Charter's scriptural root (DN 16 closing verse). The Tibetan branch's first dedicated sample now cross-branch-links to Core 2 of the project, not just Core 1.
- **Sati (mindfulness of open loops)** — noticed that CALL-FOR-HELP.md still said "only Tibetan has a first sample" — a factually incorrect statement made accurate again in the same commit.
- **Anattā (not about me)** — the Rockhill 1892 text is 134 years old; I am not adding my own voice, I am making a real old English rendering of a real Tibetan text visible under CC BY-SA 4.0 alongside its Pāli, Chinese, and dbu-can-with-Wylie context.

## What I rejected
- **Alt: pick a longer/more famous text** (e.g., full Vajracchedikā Tibetan opening). Rejected because 134 pages of Tibetan is beyond what a single daily-push turn should attempt; verses 1–4 of the Anityavarga are dense enough to be a real cross-branch sample without being cosmetic.
- **Alt: add a Sanskrit column from Bernhard 1965 as fair use**. Rejected — the whole project's rule is public-domain-only; setting a fair-use precedent even once weakens the copyright story of the entire archive. The file's `## Sources & licensing` section makes the rejection permanent and auditable.
- **Alt: use Sparham 1983 English retranslation from Tibetan** (which would be more accurate than Rockhill). Rejected for the same reason — post-cutoff, still copyright-active.
- **Alt: add a "modern English" column beside Rockhill's Victorian English**. Rejected because *that* would be a machine draft dressed up as parity with attested text; better to leave the standing gap explicit for a named reviewer.
- **Alt: do nothing, HEARTBEAT_OK**. Explicitly ruled out by cron and Prime Directive.

## Provenance
- Source: W. W. Rockhill, *Udânavarga: A Collection of Verses from the Buddhist Canon Compiled by Dharmatrāta, being the Northern Buddhist Version of Dhammapada, translated from the Tibetan of the Bkah-hgyur* (London: Trübner & Co., 1892), pp. 1–2.
- Rockhill (1854–1914) — public domain worldwide by both pre-1929 US rule and PD-old-70 (d. 1914 + 70 = 1984, PD since).
- Text: Narthang Kangyur, per Rockhill's colophon.
- Chinese parallel source: T210 《法句經》 attributed Zhi Qian, 3rd c. CE — PD by age; wording from CBETA public-facing corpus.
- Pāli parallels: PTS Dhp / Sn / DN 16 (Rhys Davids ed., pre-1929, PD).
