## Letter 1 — 俄罗斯科学院东方文献研究所 Institute of Oriental Manuscripts RAS

**To**: Институт восточных рукописей РАН (ИВР РАН) · Director's office; attn.: Dr. Shintaro Arakawa (editor of Kychanov 1999 catalogue, if still reachable there), or Dr. Kirill Solonin (current Tangut specialist at IOM RAS).

Уважаемые коллеги,

I write on behalf of **Sarasvatī**, an open-source, non-commercial archive project aiming to preserve the eight branches of the Buddhist canon under CC BY-SA 4.0 with Bitcoin-anchored integrity (OpenTimestamps + IPFS). Full details at https://github.com/lurongpan47/Sarasvati.

The IOM RAS Tangut collection from Khara-Khoto is, as Prof. Kychanov wrote, "one of the gems of the world's manuscript collections." We respect that the EAP140 digitisation grants "research only" access, and we will not mirror those images into our archive.

We write to ask:

1. **Public-domain plates**. The 1963 Gorbachëva–Kychanov *Тангутские рукописи и ксилографы* contains facsimile plates. Are these plates — or any other IOM-prepared Tangut facsimiles — already in the public domain in the Russian Federation, or under a licence that would permit CC BY-SA 4.0 inclusion in our archive?

2. **Named-reviewer collaboration**. Would any member of your Tangut group be willing to be named in our CONTRIBUTORS.md as a reviewer for **one or two short Tangut passages per quarter** that our AI first-drafts into modern Chinese / English / Tibetan? We would send the passage, the three draft columns, and specific questions (character identification, lineage attribution, dating). You accept or decline per-passage. No contract, no deadline, no commitment beyond what you choose to give.

3. **Kychanov 1999 catalogue**. The Kyoto 1999 catalogue is referenced from the Babelstone index and gandhari.org-style tooling. Is there a plan to make its full text (not only outline) openly available? If so, we would gladly link to and cite it exclusively.

Our principle is **AI first-draft, human final**. Every text we publish without a named human reviewer carries the mark ⟨བརྟག⟩ ("needs checking"). We would never publish a Tangut sample as "attested" without a Tangut scholar's name on it.

With gratitude for the century of work IOM RAS has done to keep this material alive,

Dr. Lurong Pan
