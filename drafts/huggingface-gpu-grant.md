## Part A — Space Setup (do this first)

### Suggested Space name

**Primary:** `Sarasvati/charter-reader` — "Buddhist AI Charter · 24-Language Interactive Reader"
**Alternate:** `Sarasvati/eight-branch-canon` — "Eight-Branch Buddhist Canon · Cross-Lingual Parallel Reader"

We recommend `Sarasvati/charter-reader` as the primary Space to submit for a grant: it is smaller in scope, easier to demo, and directly stress-tests the multilingual reasoning that the GPU is being requested for. `eight-branch-canon` can be a follow-on Space once the first is stable.

### Space concept

A Gradio interface that lets any visitor:

1. Load the **Buddhist AI Charter v1.1** in any of 24 languages side-by-side (ar · bo · de · es · fa · fr · hi · id · it · ja · km · ko · mn · my · pi · pt · ru · sa · si · th · tr · vi · zh-CN · zh-TW).
2. Pick any principle (ten) or refusal (six), and see how the same clause reads across 2–4 languages simultaneously, with an on-the-fly *precision-translation suggestion* generated by a mid-sized open-weight model (Qwen2.5-72B-Instruct or Llama-3.3-70B-Instruct) fine-tuned prompt for cross-lingual charter fidelity.
3. Query the charter with natural-language questions ("What does *upekkhā* forbid an AI from doing?", "Give me the six refusals in Tibetan and Pāli side-by-side", "Is this proposed clause a violation of Principle 4?") — the model answers grounded strictly in the current charter text plus, if requested, the DN 16 four-language reading (Pāli / English / Chinese / Tibetan) that is the scriptural root.
4. Flag suspected mistranslations in any i18n version and open a corresponding GitHub Issue in the [main Sarasvatī repo](https://github.com/lurongpan47/Sarasvati) with one click, seeding a reviewer pipeline.

The Space is **read-only for the charter itself** — no editing surface. This is deliberate. The Space is a *reader*, not a *rewriter*. All authoritative changes flow through the git repository under Dr. Pan's decision.

### Model choice and why a GPU is needed

CPU-only inference on models this size (~70 B parameters) is functionally unusable — a single side-by-side rendering of one principle across four languages would take minutes. A ZeroGPU or dedicated A10G / A100 slot lets us keep latency under ~5 seconds per query, which is the minimum for interactive philological reading.

We considered lighter models (Qwen2.5-7B, Llama-3.2-3B). They are adequate for majority-language rendering but drop *hard* on Pāli, Sanskrit IAST, Tibetan Uchen, Sinhala, Burmese, Khmer, and Gāndhārī reconstruction — exactly the languages the charter and canon most need to serve. This has been informally validated in our own cross-model red-team (Aug 2026, 860 evaluations); mid-size (~70 B) open-weight models cross the usability threshold, small models do not.

#### What this Space does

An interactive, cross-lingual reader for the Buddhist AI Charter v1.1 (also usable for the eight-branch canon parallels in a follow-on version). Visitors can:

- View any of the ten principles or six refusals across **2–4 of the charter's 24 languages** side-by-side.

CPU-only inference on ~70 B parameter models makes this Space unusable — a single 4-language principle rendering takes minutes and any live QA is impossible. A **ZeroGPU** slot would be ideal for our access pattern (bursty, single-visitor at a time, ~2–5 s per query). If ZeroGPU is not appropriate, a dedicated **A10G** or **A100** slot at any tier would let us keep the Space stable during expected academic-community bursts (mailing-list posts, conference references).
