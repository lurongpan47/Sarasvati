### English · Konow 1916 p. 276 (attested in-image; verses [1]–[7]) and OCR p. 277 for [8]–[10]

> **TRANSLATION.**
>
> **[Verse 1]** In three ways I bow down to the buddhas of the three ages,² with faith … I bow down to the law of three vehicles, and also, in three ways, to the order of mendicants.
>
> **[Verse 2]** In the same way I bow down to the sūtra, the prajñāpāramitā of the buddhas, the mother of all the pāramitās, well established, deep, pleasing, exalted.
>
> **[Verse 3]** The first and highest of the life of enlightenment,³ the essence of all the dharmas, which … in yoga (?).
>
> **[Verse 4]** Where there is no … … as the dharmakāya.
>
> **[Verse 5]** This prajñāpāramitā the omniscient Buddha put together ; and when one recites and explains this triśatika⁴ called Vajracchedikā,
>
> **[Verse 6]** It altogether clears away all particles (?) of karma and āvaraṇa sins as a thunderbolt. Therefore its name is Vajracchedikā (thunderbolt cutter).
>
> **[Verse 7]** Whatever the law of the buddhas may be, all that is concentrated in this sūtra, in the Vajracchedikā ; therefore it is so pleasing and exalted.

*Konow's foot-notes p. 276 (image-attested):*
> ¹ Superfluous sign of interpunctuation.
> ² Present, past, and future.
> ³ i. e. *bodhicaryā*.
> ⁴ Loanword, meaning, ' consisting of 300 granthas '.

> **[Verse 8]** ⟨OCR⟩ Whosoever learns and preserves and reads it, and causes it to be written, by him the entire Law will be grasped, and, after death, he goes … in bliss.
>
> **[Verse 9]** ⟨OCR⟩ He whose merit is great, when these sūtras are recited, by that prudent man … should be altogether made by this sūtra.
>
> **[Verse 10]** ⟨OCR⟩ Therefore I start on the exposition of it, in love of the Law and with faith, if the buddhas will give me protection so that I can preach it so that it may be grasped.

### 现代汉语 (Sarasvatī draft of Konow's English ⟨བརྟག⟩)

> **【第一偈】** 我以三种方式，怀着信心，向三世诸佛致敬……我向三乘之法致敬，又以三种方式向僧伽致敬。
> **【第二偈】** 我同样向诸佛的般若波罗蜜经致敬——它是一切波罗蜜之母，善立、深邃、悦人、崇高。
> **【第三偈】** 菩提行之首要与最胜，一切法之精髓，于瑜伽（?）中……
> **【第四偈】** 其中无有……犹如法身。
> **【第五偈】** 一切智者佛，将此般若波罗蜜汇集；凡诵读、宣说此名为金刚断的三百颂者，
> **【第六偈】** 他能彻底清除一切业与业障之罪，如金刚雷霆。故其名为金刚断。
> **【第七偈】** 诸佛之法，一切皆集此经，集此金刚断中；故此经如是悦人、如是崇高。
> **【第八偈】**⟨OCR 底本⟩ 凡学持读写此经者，他能摄受全部正法；命终之后，往生……安乐处。
> **【第九偈】**⟨OCR 底本⟩ 若人福大，闻此等经时，智者……当由此经成就一切。
> **【第十偈】**⟨OCR 底本⟩ 故我以法爱与净信开讲此经；若诸佛护念我，使我宣说、使之被摄受，我即如所应宣说而说。

## Reading notes · typography & provenance

**Konow's interlinear layout** (p. 239 image-attested): Khotanese set in larger roman type on each line; Sanskrit back-translation directly below each Khotanese line in **smaller** roman type with each Sanskrit word placed under the Khotanese word it corresponds to; gaps are left where there is no equivalent (e.g. `Bodhi-` sits under `baysīṁñe` but nothing sits under `karä`). The format `[1*b*ⁱ]` means MS folio 1 verso, line i (`b` is italic for *verso*; `a` is italic for *recto*; superscript roman i, ii, iii, iv number the MS's four lines per side). Dots `•` and colons `:` and double daṇḍa `‖` reproduce the MS's own punctuation. Verse numbers (plain `2`, `3` or bracketed `[1]`, `[4]`, `[5]`, `[6]`, `[7]`) sit in the right margin of the Khotanese line that ends the verse. Konow uses `(?)` to mark Sanskrit back-translations he is unsure of (e.g. `yoge (?)` for `yauga`), and blanks or dots in the back-translation where he could not identify a Sanskrit equivalent for a Khotanese word.

**English page 276 layout** (image-attested): the ten introductory verses are in English prose, numbered `[Verse 1]`, `[Verse 2]`, …, each verse in bracketed form at line head. Lacunae are shown as `. . .` (three spaced dots) or `. . . . .` (five dots) corresponding to the Khotanese places Konow could not translate (not to MS damage per se — the "lacunae" are of understanding, not of text). Footnotes `²`, `³`, `⁴` key superscripts in the body to the four foot-notes at the page bottom.

**DjVu OCR drift note**: The Cornell IA OCR renders Konow's diacritics loosely — `b̮aysä` as `baysa`, `ṣadi-jsa` as `sadi-jsa`, superscript Roman numerals as inline `ii`/`iii`, the italic *b* of folio markers as plain. For verses [1]–[7] above the Khotanese column was corrected against the image of p. 239; for verses [8]–[10] the column is still OCR-based with Konow's p. 239 typography carried as a template. The reviewer should treat the ⟨OCR⟩-marked lines as a starting point for a next-session image-read of p. 240.

**Lift provenance** for Sanskrit / English-of-the-Sanskrit / Chinese: these three columns are copied *attested* from the 2026-10-19 sibling file `translations/vajracchedika/sections-1-2-4lang.md`, which was itself scan-first built from Müller 1881 pp. 19–20 (IA `p1anecdotaoxonie01oxfouoft`), Müller 1894 SBE XLIX pp. 111–112 (IA `sacredbooksoftheeastvol49_202002`) and the Taishō T235 (IA `taisho-tripitaka` leaf `_0747`). Those scans were not re-opened today; the columns are the sibling file's text verbatim.

**The point of this file**: for the first time in Sarasvatī, a Buddhist text can be read side-by-side in **Khotanese** (an Iranian language carrying Mahāyāna Buddhism through Central Asia from roughly the 5th to 10th century CE), **Sanskrit** (the Indian source), **Chinese** (the Kumārajīva translation that defined East Asian Buddhism), with **English translations by both Konow 1916 and Müller 1894 for cross-check**. The Khotanese witness is independent of the Sanskrit traditions preserved in India and Nepal — Konow's manuscript was found at the "Caves of the Thousand Buddhas" at Dunhuang by Sir Aurel Stein in 1907 — and it is one of only two complete Vajracchedikā manuscripts in an Iranian language (the other is Tibetan). The Khotanese translator's choices — "the Venerable among Venerables" for Bhagavat, "the law of three vehicles" for the triyāna, the verb pattern that puts the ten introductory verses *before* the sūtra proper (Konow p. 215, "the manuscript commences with a long introduction in the 'unknown language', of which there is no Sanskrit equivalent in Max Müller's edition") — are the Silk Road's own voice making the Diamond-Cutter into something readable to a Khotanese-speaking Buddhist community a thousand years ago.
