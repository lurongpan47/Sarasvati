## ⚠ Erratum (2026-09-24) — Chinese column re-read against the Taishō leaves; it was the popular recension, not the Taishō text

The 2026-09-13 version of this file (commit `ca3fb61`) labelled its Chinese column "原文 T389" and cited "Taishō vol. 12 no. 389". Read against the leaves, the column is the modern **流通本** wording of the 佛遺教經 — the Ming-line text that circulates in temple editions and online — not the Taishō main text. It agrees with the Taishō in some 99 % of its characters, but at **six** places it differs, and at **four** of those six it prints exactly the reading that the Taishō editors relegate to their foot apparatus as the 宋・元・明 (and 宮) variant: 涯 for 崖 (n. ③ 崖＝涯㊒㊪), the omission of 善 in 諸善功德 (n. ㉑〔善〕－㊂㊝), 說 for 欲 in 所欲利益 (n. ⑤ 欲＝說㊂), 道 for 導 in 善導 (n. ⑧ 導＝道㊂㊝). The other two (宜 for 應; the dropped 若 before 種種戲論) are in no Taishō note at all. The 09-13 file also gave no page, column or line, carried none of the apparatus, and printed modern punctuation (，；、) as if it were on the page.

The column is now the printed Taishō text, one Taishō column per line with its line number (`a08` = p. 1111 register a, column 8, i.e. `T12n0389_p1111a08`), 17 characters to the column as set. Conventions: (i) **glyph forms are as printed** — 爲, 敎, 愼, 鉤 (the 09-13 column had 為, 教, 慎); (ii) **punctuation is the Taishō's own**: `。` where the leaf shows a closed ring, `、` where the mark read as a short tick at 3–5× — the two are hard to separate on this scan and the tick may be a broken ring, so `、` means "mark of uncertain shape", not a claim that the editors used a 讀點 there; marks the reads disagreed on are listed in the review section; (iii) the **circled numerals ③④⑤⑥㉑④⑤⑥⑦⑧** stand where the leaf prints them and the notes are carried under each passage as read from the foot; (iv) the 09-13 selection's **ellipses …… are kept**, and the elided text is printed in 〔 〕 in the column so that every cut can be seen to remove text only and join nothing that was not adjacent — both cuts in Passage I and the one in Passage III are honest by that test; (v) the two half-size lines of the title block (①亦名佛遺敎經, ②後秦龜茲國三藏鳩摩羅什 / 奉　詔譯) are given as set.

## Passage I · 制心一處 · Fixing the mind on a single point

### Chinese · 原文 · Taishō T12n0389 as printed (鳩摩羅什 譯)
> **T12n0389_p1111a08–a20** (IA leaf 1110, register a, columns 8–20)
>
> a08 汝等比丘。已能住戒當制五根。勿令放逸入
> a09 於五欲。譬如牧牛之人執杖視之。不令縱逸
> a10 犯人苗稼。若縱五根。非唯五欲將無③崖畔不
> a11 可制也。亦如惡馬不以轡制。將當牽人墜於

> a14 持之如賊不令縱逸。假令縱之。皆亦不久見
> a15 其磨滅。此五根者心爲其主。是故汝等當

### English (Sarasvatī machine draft ⟨བརྟག⟩)
> Bhikṣus, you who already stand firm in the precepts must next restrain the five faculties, letting them fall neither into laxity nor into the five desires. As a cowherd holds his staff and watches over his cattle so they neither wander nor trample another's crops, so must you watch the faculties. Let them run loose, and it is not the five desires alone that grow — they become boundless and can no longer be checked; like a bad horse ungoverned by bit and rein, they will drag the rider down into the pit. … For this reason the wise restrain and do not follow them, holding them as one holds a thief, giving them no room to run wild; and if for a moment they are let go, one soon sees them exhausted. Of these five faculties, the mind is master; therefore you must guard the mind well. The dreadfulness of the mind exceeds a poisonous snake, a savage beast, a hateful bandit or a great fire breaking free — no simile suffices. … Fix it on a single point, and there is no task that cannot be accomplished.

### 現代白話中文 (Sarasvatī 機譯 ⟨བརྟག⟩)
> 諸位比丘，你們既然已能安住於戒律，接下來就要收攝眼耳鼻舌身這五種感官，不要放縱它們去追逐五種欲望。就像牧牛人手持棍杖看著牛，不讓牠亂跑去踐踏別人的莊稼一樣。如果放任五根，不僅是五欲，連煩惱本身也會漫無邊際、再也管不住；就像一匹惡馬沒有韁繩，會把騎的人拖進坑裡。……所以有智慧的人是收攝而不隨順，把它們當作賊一樣看牢，不給它們胡來的空間；就算偶爾放了一下，也很快就看到它們自己耗盡。這五種感官裡，心才是主人，所以你們一定要把心看好。心的可怕，遠遠超過毒蛇、猛獸、怨敵、失控的大火——任何比喻都不夠形容。……只要把心安住在一處，沒有什麼事辦不成。

## Passage II · 少欲知足 · Few desires, contentment

### Chinese · 原文 · Taishō T12n0389 as printed (鳩摩羅什 譯)
> **T12n0389_p1111b28–c10** (IA leaf 1110, register b columns 28–29, register c columns 1–10)
>
> b28 汝等比丘。當知多欲之人。多求利故苦惱亦
> b29 多。少欲之人無求無欲則無此患。直爾少欲
> c01 尚應修習。何況少欲能生諸㉑善功德。少欲之
> c02 人則無諂曲以求人意。亦復不爲諸根所牽。
> c03 行少欲者心則坦然無所憂畏。觸事有餘常
> c04 無不足、有少欲者則有涅槃。是名少欲
> c05 汝等比丘。若欲脫諸苦惱。當觀知足。知足之
> c06 法即是富樂安隱之處。知足之人雖臥地上
> c07 猶爲安樂。不知足者雖處天堂亦不稱意。不
> c08 知足者雖富而貧。知足之人雖貧而富。不知
> c09 足者常爲五欲所牽。爲知足者之所憐愍。是
> c10 名知足
>
> *Taishō foot apparatus, p. 1111:* ㉑ 〔善〕－㊂㊝
>
> *Read:* the 09-13 selection took b28–c10 whole; no ellipsis. c04 and c10 are short columns as set (the section ends at 是名少欲 / 是名知足 and the next 汝等比丘 starts a fresh column).

### English (Sarasvatī machine draft ⟨བརྟག⟩)
> Bhikṣus, know this: the one with many desires, because of much seeking after gain, has much suffering as well; the one with few desires, having nothing to seek and nothing to crave, is free of this affliction. Even bare fewness of desire is worth cultivating — how much more so when fewness of desire itself produces every virtue. One of few desires does not flatter or twist himself to win over others' minds, nor is he dragged about by the sense-faculties. Whoever practises few desires — his mind lies open and even, without worry or fear; whatever situation he meets, he has enough to spare and is never in want. Where there is fewness of desire, there is nirvāṇa. This is what is called few desires.
>
> Bhikṣus, if you wish to be freed from all suffering and vexation, contemplate contentment. The teaching of knowing-enough is itself the very place of wealth, joy and peace. One who knows enough, though he lie on the bare ground, is still at ease; one who does not know enough, though he dwell in a heavenly palace, is still not satisfied. He who does not know enough is poor even while rich; he who knows enough is rich even while poor. The unsatisfied are forever dragged by the five desires, and are pitied by those who know enough. This is what is called contentment.

### 現代白話中文 (Sarasvatī 機譯 ⟨བརྟག⟩)
> 諸位比丘，你們要明白：欲望多的人，因為求取利益多，所以煩惱也多；欲望少的人，沒什麼可求也沒什麼可貪，就沒有這種苦惱。就算只是單純地少欲，本身就值得修習，何況少欲還能生出種種功德。少欲的人，不會為了迎合別人而諂媚彎腰，也不會被感官牽著跑。修少欲的人，心裡坦坦然然，沒有憂慮和恐懼，遇到任何事情都覺得綽綽有餘，永遠不會覺得不夠。哪裡有少欲，哪裡就有涅槃——這就叫做「少欲」。
>
> 諸位比丘，如果想擺脫一切苦惱，就應該觀「知足」。知足這件事，本身就是富足、快樂、安穩的所在。知足的人，就算睡在地上也覺得安樂；不知足的人，就算住在天宮也不稱心。不知足的人，雖富卻窮；知足的人，雖窮卻富。不知足的人，永遠被五欲牽著走，是知足的人所憐憫的對象——這就叫做「知足」。

### English (Sarasvatī machine draft ⟨བརྟག⟩)
> Bhikṣus, if you strive with diligence, then nothing is too hard. Therefore you must strive with diligence. As a small stream, if it flows on and on, can wear a hole through stone, so too must the practitioner's mind not slacken again and again. If the mind keeps slackening, it is like drilling for fire and stopping before it grows hot: however much one wants the fire, fire is hard to get. This is what is called diligence.
>
> Bhikṣus, all kinds of frivolous talk throw the mind into confusion; even one who has gone forth from home is, in that state, not yet freed. Therefore, bhikṣus, you must quickly cast off the confused mind and its frivolous talk. If you wish to gain the joy of quiet cessation, you have only to skilfully extinguish the affliction of frivolous talk — this is what is called non-frivolous-talk.
>
> Bhikṣus, in every virtue you must always keep the mind one-pointed and cast off every heedlessness, as one leaves a hated bandit. All the benefits spoken by the greatly-compassionate World-honoured One are now brought to their end; you have only to be diligent in practising them. … I am like a skilful physician: knowing the illness I have set forth the medicine — whether you take it or not is no fault of the physician. I am also like a good guide: I have shown the good road — if the one who hears does not walk it, that is no fault of the guide.
