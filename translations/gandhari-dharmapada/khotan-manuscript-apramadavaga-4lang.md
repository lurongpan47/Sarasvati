## About this file

This is the **attested-text** first sample for the Silk Road branch. Unlike the sibling file `khotan-fragment-Ia-3lang.md`, which presents Dhp 1–4 in a Gāndhārī column *reconstructed from the Pāli* (because those verses are not preserved in the surviving Khotan fragments), the Gāndhārī column below reproduces actual manuscript readings — as transcribed by Émile Senart in 1897 (*Journal Asiatique*) and re-edited by Benimadhab Barua & Sailendranath Mitra in *Prakrit Dhammapada, based upon M. Senart's Kharoṣṭhī manuscript* (University of Calcutta, 1921).

- **Gāndhārī column**: exact readings from Barua–Mitra 1921 (which reproduces Senart 1897 with re-adjustments), **checked against the scan on 2026-09-20** (Internet Archive `in.ernet.dli.2015.41588`; Part I = Senart's plate-order text with his notes in English translation, Part II = Barua–Mitra's rearranged text with Pāli/Udānavarga parallels). Both editions are in the public domain (US: pre-1929 publication with no visible copyright notice; India: PD by age). This is **not** a reconstruction. Round-bracketed letters are Senart's / Barua–Mitra's own editorial completions of damaged akṣaras, reproduced as they print them; `○` is the manuscript's verse-end circle (B–M mark where they supply it). Verse numbers below follow Barua–Mitra's rearranged Chapter 2 (*Apramādavaga*, 25 stanzas per the colophon `ga 25`); Senart's plate/line references are given alongside.
- **English column**: Sarasvatī machine draft, primary source Pāli Dhammapada parallels; not scholar-reviewed.
- **Chinese column**: Sarasvatī machine draft, cross-checked against T210 《法句經》*Faju jing* (Zhi Qian et al., 3rd c. CE, PD by age) for parallel wording where the *Faju jing* preserves the same verse.
- **Pāli parallel column**: PTS Dhammapada, public domain.

**Why the Apramādavaga.** The opening chapter of the Khotan manuscript, called *Magavaga* (Path chapter) by Senart's re-arrangement, is fragmentary and hard to read without the plates. The second chapter, *Apramādavaga* (Diligence chapter), preserves multiple whole ślokas with clear Pāli parallels in Dhp 27, 30, 167, and 327. That gives us four points at which the same verse can be triangulated across Kharoṣṭhī Gāndhārī, Pāli, Chinese, and English — which is exactly what this archive exists to do.

**What is still a draft.** Every non-Gāndhārī column below is a Sarasvatī machine draft. The Gāndhārī column is a **reproduction of a real manuscript transcription**, not a draft — but the English and Chinese renderings still need a comparative Buddhist philologist's eye. Every uncertain rendering is marked ⟨བརྟག⟩.

## Verse 1 · Barua–Mitra Apramādavaga v. 20 (Part II p. 134) · Senart plate A¹ l. 2 · parallel to Pāli Dhp 27 · "Delight in diligence"

### Gāndhārī (Khotan ms., plate A¹ l. 2; Senart 1897 = B–M 1921 Part I p. 1, Part II v. 20 p. 134)
> apramadi pramodia ma gami ratisabhamu
> apramato hi jhayatu viseṣa adhikachati ○

*(Kharoṣṭhī orthography preserved. `°adhikachati` = Pāli `adhigacchati`, the `-cch-` cluster palatalizing to `-kach-` in Gāndhārī sound-shift; `viseṣa` = Skt `viśeṣa` "distinction, excellence" — Senart's note d rejects reading a final anusvāra, `viseṣaṃ`. Senart's note b: `gami` (= Pāli `gāme`, "in the village") is "so obviously preferable" to Pāli `kāma-` that he suspects the Pāli is the corrupt one. The very next line of the folio, A¹ l. 3 = B–M v. 21, repeats the first half and ends `apramato hi jhayatu chaya dukhasa pramuni ○`, "…attains the destruction of suffering".)*

### English (Sarasvatī draft from Pāli)
> Delight in diligence; do not indulge in pleasure and sensual gatherings. The diligent one, meditating, attains supreme excellence.

### Chinese (Sarasvatī draft; cf. T210《法句經·放逸品》)
> 当乐于精勤，勿溺于欲乐、放逸之会。
> 精勤而禅思者，能证殊胜之境。

### Pāli parallel (Dhp 27, PTS)
> Mā pamādamanuyuñjetha, mā kāmaratisanthavaṃ;
> appamatto hi jhāyanto, pappoti vipulaṃ sukhaṃ.

## Verse 2 · Barua–Mitra Apramādavaga v. 11 (Part II pp. 128–129) · Senart plates A³ l. 17 + A² l. 1 joined · parallel to Pāli Dhp 30 · "By diligence Maghavā rose to lordship"

### Gāndhārī (Khotan ms., B–M 1921 Part II v. 11 pp. 128–129, joining Senart's A³ l. 17 + A² l. 1)
> apra(madena) makabha devana samidh(i) gatu
> apramada prasajhati pramadu garahitu sada ○

*(Barua & Mitra's key restoration — "a momentous event in the compilation of the present edition" (p. 129): Senart's A³ l. 17 reads `apra…` + `apramada prasajhati pramadu garahitu sada`, and his A² l. 1 reads `. . madenamakabha devanasamidh(i)gat .`, a fragment he could match to no Pāli verse; B–M saw that the foot of A³ and the head of A² are one stanza. `makabha` = Pāli `Maghavā` (Indra): B–M, "the aspiration of *b* compensates for the change of *gh* into *k* through an intermediate *g*". `samidhi` = Pāli `samiddhiṃ`, substituting for `seṭṭhataṃ` ("went to prosperity/pre-eminence among the gods"). `prasajhati` = Pāli `pasaṃsanti`: Senart's note a on A³ l. 17 — "the *j* is surmounted by a horizontal stroke which appears to mark the aspirate … there is no doubt about the reading" (cf. `prañaj(h)ati`, B l. 21); B–M add that one "should have expected a reading like *prasaṃsati*", which is exactly why this file must print what the folio has, not what one expects. `gatu`: final akṣara supplied by B–M.)*

### English (Sarasvatī draft from Pāli)
> By diligence Maghavā rose to lordship among the gods. Diligence is praised, negligence forever blamed.

### Chinese (Sarasvatī draft; cf. T210《法句經·放逸品》"戒为甘露道，放逸为死径")
> 由精勤故，摩伽婆得升诸天之首。
> 精勤为世所赞，放逸为世所诃。

### Pāli parallel (Dhp 30, PTS)
> Appamādena Maghavā, devānaṃ seṭṭhataṃ gato;
> appamādaṃ pasaṃsanti, pamādo garahito sadā.

## Verse 3 · Barua–Mitra Apramādavaga v. 23 (Part II p. 137) · Senart plate A¹ l. 5 · parallel to Pāli Dhp 327 · "Draw yourself up out of the mire"

### Gāndhārī (Khotan ms., plate A¹ l. 5; Senart 1897 = B–M 1921 Part I p. 3, Part II v. 23 p. 137)
> apramadarata bhodha sadhami supravedite
> drugha udhvaradha atmana pagasana va kuñ(aru) ○

*(The folio preserves only `kuñ` of the last word; Senart, note b: "in spite of partial mutilation, the last letter is certain; it is kuñ[jaro] that ought to be read, or, what recurs in the same way, kuñaru, which we shall presently find again in A³, 4" — B–M print `kuñ(aru)`, "supplied by us". `drugha` = Pāli `duggā` "from the evil way / the mire" (Senart: the *r* transposed in writing, cf. `udhvaradha` for `uddharadha`; B–M: "seems to have reference to 'mud-forts'"); `udhvaradha` = Pāli `uddharetha`, imperative "lift up, draw out"; `sadhami supravedite` = Pāli `saddhamme suppavedite`, "in the well-proclaimed good Dhamma" — the Pāli Dhp 327 has `sacittamanurakkhatha` here instead, so the second foot is a Gāndhārī variant, not a match. B–M note the whole stanza is "a new combination" of set Pāli phrases; its closest Pāli is Dhp 327 for the second line only.)*

### English (Sarasvatī draft from Pāli)
> Take delight in diligence in the well-taught Dhamma. Draw yourselves up out of the mire — as an elephant sunk in mud draws itself out.

### Chinese (Sarasvatī draft; cf. T210《法句經·放逸品》"当自克修，随其教训")
> 于善说之法，当乐于精进。
> 自拔离恶泥，犹象出淤泥。

### Pāli parallel (Dhp 327, PTS)
> Appamādaratā hotha, sacittamanurakkhatha;
> duggā uddharathattānaṃ, paṅke sanno'va kuñjaro.

## Verse 4 · Barua–Mitra Apramādavaga v. 12 (Part II p. 129) · Senart plate A² l. 2 · parallel to Pāli Dhp 167 · "Do not follow low doctrine"

### Gāndhārī (Khotan ms., plate A² l. 2; Senart 1897 = B–M 1921 Part I, Part II v. 12 p. 129 — three akṣaras damaged; round brackets are Senart's / B–M's completions from Dhp 167)
> (hi)na dhama na sev(e)a pramadena na savasi
> michadithi na roy(e)a na sia lokavadhano

*(Senart's A² l. 2 as printed: `. na dhama na sev . a pramadena na savasi / michadithi na roy . na sia lokavadhano`; his note a: "the comparison with Dh. 167 enables us to complete [hi]na and, probably, sev[e]a". `hina dhama` = Pāli `hīnaṃ dhammaṃ`, "low / mean doctrine"; `sevea` / `royea` = Pāli `seveyya` / `roceyya` (Gāndhārī `-eyya` → `-ea`, cf. `pramodia` in Verse 1); B–M, p. 129: the verse "is exactly similar in expression to its Pāli counterpart except for *royea* (= *roceyya*) in place of *seveyya*" in the third foot — the Pāli repeats `seveyya`, the Gāndhārī has "approve / take pleasure in" (Senart compares `imaṃ dhammaṃ roceti`, Fausbøll's *Dhammapada* p. 122). `michadithi` = Pāli `micchādiṭṭhiṃ`, "wrong view"; `lokavadhano` = Pāli `lokavaḍḍhano`, "one who increases the world" — Senart's note c reads it as a counsel against increasing the number of beings, i.e. against desire and rebirth. No verse-end circle is printed by B–M here.)*

### English (Sarasvatī draft from Pāli)
> Do not follow a mean doctrine. Do not dwell in negligence. Do not embrace wrong view. Do not be one who prolongs the world.

### Chinese (Sarasvatī draft; cf. T210《法句經·放逸品》)
> 勿从卑劣之法，勿住放逸；
> 勿怀邪见，勿为长养世间者。

### Pāli parallel (Dhp 167, PTS)
> Hīnaṃ dhammaṃ na seveyya, pamādena na saṃvase;
> micchādiṭṭhiṃ na roceyya, na siyā lokavaḍḍhano.

## Historical note

The Dutreuil de Rhins manuscript was recovered in 1892 near Khotan (Tarim Basin, modern Xinjiang) by members of Jules-Léon Dutreuil de Rhins's Central Asian expedition. It was split between Paris (Bibliothèque nationale, the Rhins portion) and St Petersburg (Petroffsky portion, adjusted by Serge d'Oldenbourg). Émile Senart published the first edition in *Journal Asiatique* 1897, creating a sensation at the Paris Oriental Congress: it was the first known Buddhist manuscript in a Prakrit dialect (Gāndhārī), in a script (Kharoṣṭhī) previously known only from North-West Indian inscriptions. Barua & Mitra's 1921 University of Calcutta edition re-adjusted Senart's plates, filling gaps and identifying additional Pāli parallels; that work is what this file draws on.

For a full modern re-edition, see John Brough, *The Gāndhārī Dharmapada* (London 1962) — still under copyright, so not reproduced here. Later Kharoṣṭhī manuscript finds (British Library scrolls, Senior collection, Split collection, Bajaur collection, all published 2000s–2020s by the Early Buddhist Manuscripts Project at the University of Washington) have vastly expanded the Gāndhārī corpus, but the Khotan Dhammapada remains the oldest and most-studied Gāndhārī literary text.
