*Major Rock Edict XIII · Kālsī recension · 4-language reading · India branch second sample*

**Sources**: Prakrit (Kālsī rock, as edited in E. Hultzsch, *Inscriptions of Asoka*, New Edition = *Corpus Inscriptionum Indicarum* vol. **Branch**: India · 印度源流 — second sample (extends the branch beyond Edict XII). Aśoka's Major Rock Edict XIII (c. 256–255 BCE) is the single most historically decisive Ashokan text: the king's public repentance for the conquest of Kaliṅga, the announcement that henceforth only *dhamma-vijaya* (conquest by dharma) is legitimate, and the earliest surviving South Asian document to name contemporary Hellenistic kings.

**Recension note (corrected 2026-09-22)**: Kālsī is the recension edited here because it is in a Brāhmī-script Eastern Prakrit that stands close to the Aśokan chancery language. It is **not** complete: the first four lines of the south face are almost entirely lost (Hultzsch prints dotted lacunae; sentences (L)–(N) do not survive at all, and (O)–(P) are fragmentary). Hultzsch's square brackets mark akṣaras that are damaged or supplied by him; his dotted runs mark akṣaras lost; his proposed restorations and emendations stand in the footnotes, **not** in the text, and he does not fill Kālsī's gaps from Shāhbāzgaṛhī or Girnar in the text itself. Both conventions are kept below.

## Passage I · The Kaliṅga war  (Hultzsch A–B)

### Prakrit (Kālsī, east face, line 35; Hultzsch 1925 p. 45)
> (A) aṭha-[va]shā- | bhishita-¹ | shā [De]vānaṁpiyasha Piyadashine | lājine | Kaligyā vijitā | (B) diyaḍha-mite² | pāna-shat[a]-shaha[ś]e | ye [ta]phā apavuḍhe [| śa]ta-[sha]hasha-mite³ | tata hate | bahu-tāvatake⁴ | vā maṭe

¹ Hultzsch n. 1: "*bhisita-* Bühler." — ² n. 2: "*-m[ā]te* Bühler." — ³ n. 3: "*-māte* Bühler." — ⁴ n. 4: "*-tāvaṁtake* Bühler."

### English (Hultzsch 1925 p. 47)
> (A) When king Dēvānāṁpriya Priyadarśin had been anointed eight years, (the country of) the Kaliṅgyas was conquered by (him). (B) One hundred and fifty thousand in number were the men who were deported thence, one hundred thousand in number were those who were slain there, and many times as many those who died.

## Passage II · The king's remorse  (Hultzsch C–F)

### Prakrit (Kālsī, east face, lines 35–36; Hultzsch 1925 p. 45)
> (C) tat[o⁵ pa]chhā | adhunā ladhesha⁶ | Kaligyeshu | tive | dhaṁma[vāy]e dhaṁma-k[ā]matā | dhaṁmānushathi chā | Devānaṁpiyashā | (D) sh[e] athi anushaye | Devānaṁpiya[sh]ā | vijin[i]tu⁷ | Kaligyāni | (E) avijitaṁ hi | vijinamane | e tatā | vadha⁸ vā | malane vā | apavahe [vā] | jan[a]shā | [sh]e bāḍha | vedaniya-mute | g[u]l[u]-mut[e] chā | Devānaṁ[pi]yashā | (F) iyaṁ pi chu | tato | galu-matatale | D[e]vānaṁpiyashā⁹

⁵ Hultzsch n. 5: "*tatā* Senart and Bühler." — ⁶ n. 6: "Read *ladheshu*, which is Bühler's reading." — ⁷ n. 7: "This word may be read also *vijiniti*, as both an *i* and a *u* are affixed to the last consonant." — ⁸ n. 8: "*vadhaṁ* Bühler." — ⁹ n. 9: "Bühler adds |."

### English (Hultzsch 1925 p. 47)
> (C) After that, now that (the country of) the Kaliṅgyas has been taken, Dēvānāṁpriya (is devoted) to a zealous study of morality, to the love of morality, and to the instruction (of people) in morality. (D) This is the repentance of Dēvānāṁpriya on account of his conquest of (the country of) the Kaliṅgyas. (E) For, this is considered¹ very painful and deplorable by Dēvānāṁpriya, that, while one is conquering² an unconquered (country), slaughter, death, and deportation of people (are taking place) there. (F) But the following is considered even more deplorable than this by Dēvānāṁpriya.

¹ Hultzsch n. 1 (p. 47): "For *muta* = *mata* see above, p. 35, n. 10." — ² n. 2: "*vijinamane* is a nominative singular absolute; see my note on the translation of the Dhauli separate edict I, S."

### Chinese (Sarasvatī machine draft ⟨བརྟག⟩)
> 自征服羯陵伽以来，天爱王于正法一意研习、爱敬、并令人民修学。天爱王为征服羯陵伽事而生深切悔痛。凡征未服之国，杀戮、死亡、俘徙一民于当地，天爱王皆视为极痛切、极沉重之事。然更有较此为重者。

## Passage III · The wound spreads beyond soldiers  (Hultzsch G–I)

### Prakrit (Kālsī, east face, lines 37–38; Hultzsch 1925 p. 45)
> (G) [ya] tatā¹⁰ vashati b[ā]bhanā¹¹ va shama¹² vā ane vā pāśaṁḍa gih[i]thā vā yeśu vihitā [e]sh[a] a[gabhu]ṭ[i]-shushushā¹³ m[ā]tā-piti-shushushā¹⁴ galu-shushā¹⁵ mita-shaṁthuta-shahāya-nātikeshu dāśa-bha[ṭa]kash[i¹⁶ sha]m[y]ā-paṭipati diḍha-bhatitā teshaṁ tatā hoti [upa]ghāte vā vadhe vā abhilatānaṁ vā vinikhamane (H) yeshaṁ vā pi shuvihi[t]ānaṁ¹⁷ shinehe avipahine e tānaṁ mita-śaṁth[u]ta-sha[h]āya-[nā]tikya¹⁸ viyashanaṁ¹⁹ pāpunāta²⁰ tatā²¹ she [p]i t[ā]namev[ā]²² upaghāt[e] hoti (I) paṭibhāge chā esh[a] sh[a]va-manu[shāna]ṁ gul[u]-m[a]te chā Devāna[ṁ]piyashā

Lines 37–38 carry no divider strokes on the rock; Hultzsch prints none. — ¹⁰ Hultzsch n. 10: "Read *ye tatā* in accordance with the Shāhbāzgaṛhī version, which reads *ye tatra*; *savatā* Senart and Bühler, who adds |." — ¹¹ n. 11: "*baṁbhanā* Senart and Bühler." — ¹² n. 12: "Read *shamanā*." — ¹³ n. 13: "*ag[a]bh[uta]-* Bühler." — ¹⁴ n. 14: "*matā-* Bühler." — ¹⁵ n. 15: "*-shusha* Bühler; read *-shushushā*." — ¹⁶ n. 16: "*-bha[ta]kashi* Bühler." — ¹⁷ n. 17: "*[sha]ṁvihitānaṁ* Bühler." — ¹⁸ n. 18: "The syllable *nā* seems to be entered above the line." — ¹⁹ n. 19: "*viyashane* Bühler." — ²⁰ n. 20: "Read *pāpunāti*, which is Bühler's reading." — ²¹ n. 21: "*tata* Bühler." — ²² n. 22: "*°meva* Bühler."

### English (Hultzsch 1925 p. 47)
> (G) (To) the Brāhmaṇas or Śramaṇas, or other sects or householders,³ who are living there, (and) among whom the following are practised: obedience to those who receive high pay,⁴ obedience to mother and father, obedience to elders, proper courtesy to friends, acquaintances, companions, and relatives, to slaves and servants, (and) firm devotion,—to these then happen injury or slaughter or deportation of (their) beloved ones. **Historical note** (editorial, not from the scan; corrected 2026-09-22). Rock Edict XIII survives at Kālsī (Uttarakhand), at Shāhbāzgaṛhī and Mānsehrā (Kharoṣṭhī script, Pakistan), in fragments at Girnar (Gujarat), and — found after Hultzsch went to press — at Yerragudi (Andhra Pradesh). Hultzsch's *Corpus Inscriptionum Indicarum* vol. I (Oxford: Clarendon Press, 1925) edits Kālsī, Shāhbāzgaṛhī, Mānsehrā and the Girnar fragments side by side and remains the standard critical edition; it is public domain and on the Internet Archive: <https://archive.org/details/InscriptionsOfAsoka.NewEditionByE.Hultzsch>. On the Kālsī rock the edict runs across two faces: east face ll. 35–39 (sentences A–K) and south face ll. 1–18 (O–AA, the first four lines largely lost, so that L–N are missing and O–P fragmentary). Hultzsch's page layout for Kālsī XIII: heading and Devanāgarī text from p. 43 (l. 35) through p. 44 to the top of p. 45; Roman transliteration pp. 45–46; "Translation" pp. 47–49; the photographic plate "KALSI ROCK; EAST FACE" (scale one-sixth) is an unnumbered leaf facing p. 44.

The four kings named after Antiyoga in (Q) — Tulamaya, Antekina, Makā, Alikyashudala — are identified in Hultzsch's n. 6 (p. 48) as Ptolemy II Philadelphos of Egypt (285–247), Antigonus Gonatas of Macedonia (276–239), Magas of Cyrene (c. 300–250), and Alexander of Epirus (272–c. 255) or "more probably" Alexander of Corinth (252–c. 244); Antiyoga is Antiochus II of Syria (n. 2). The synchronism is the most secure chronological anchor for Aśoka's reign, placing REXIII c. 256–255 BCE, and the edict is the earliest attested document of Hellenistic–Indian diplomatic contact.
