This is Aśoka's own epigraphic witness to his pilgrimage, in regnal year 20 (c. 249 BCE), to the place "here the Buddha was born" — the only epigraphic witness naming a historical monarch at Lumbinī and the earliest dated inscription anywhere calling the Buddha `Sakyamunī`.

## Reading conventions

- Hultzsch's transliteration conventions are kept: *ch* = c, *chh* = ch, *ṁ* = anusvāra, square brackets `[ ]` = akṣaras damaged or supplied, superscript numerals = his notes. His **heavy type** (Clarendon) is kept in bold where it falls (the names **Devāna[pi]yena Piyadasina lājina vīsati-vasābhisitena**, **Budhe**, **Sakyamunī**, **Bhagavaṁ**, **Luṁmini-gāme**, and the sentence markers **(A) (B) (C)**); we do not interpret it.
- Line numbers **1–5** are the pillar's lines as Hultzsch prints them; sentence letters **(A)–(C)** are his. Run-overs in his layout (line 2 continues section A; line 4 contains the end of section A-echo + start of C; line 5 ends C) are reproduced as he prints them.
- Notes are quoted verbatim, then a raised dash `—` introduces any remark of ours (always so marked).

## Passage I · The king's twentieth-year pilgrimage to Lumbinī  (Hultzsch A)

### Prakrit (lines 1–2; Hultzsch 1925 p. 164)

#### Devanāgarī row (as printed)
> 1 **(A)** देवानपियेन पियदसिन लाजिन वीसतिवसाभिसितेन
> 2 अतन आगाच महीयिते हिद बुधे जाते सक्यमुनी ति

#### Roman transliteration
> 1 **(A) Devāna[pi]yena Piyadasina lājina vīsati-vasābhisitena**
> 2 atana āgācha mahīyite hida **Budhe** jāte **Sakyamunī** ti

### English (Hultzsch 1925 p. 164)
> **(A)** When **king Dēvānāṁpriya Priyadarśin** had been **anointed twenty years**, he came himself and worshipped (this spot), because **the Buddha Śākyamuni** was born here.²

² p. 164 n. 2: "In the *Mahāparinibbāna-sutta* (JRAS, 1876. 241), the Buddha himself is reported to have said: '*āgamissanti kho Ānanda saddhā bhikkhu-bhikkhuniyo upāsaka-upāsikāyo idha Tathāgato jāto ti.*' In accordance with this quotation I connect the words from *hida* to *ti* with section A, while Fleet (JRAS, 1908. 486) joined them to B."

### 现代汉语 (Sarasvatī machine draft ⟨བརྟག⟩)
> （A）**天爱喜见王**灌顶即位已满**二十年**时，亲自躬临礼敬此地，因**佛陀释迦牟尼**诞生于此。

### བོད་ཡིག (Sarasvatī machine draft ⟨བརྟག⟩)
> (A) ལྷར་དགའ་མཛེས་མཐོང་རྒྱལ་པོ་དབང་བསྐུར་ནས་ལོ་ཉི་ཤུ་འཁོར་བའི་ཚེ། སངས་རྒྱས་ཤཱཀྱ་ཐུབ་པ་འདིར་སྐུ་བལྟམས་པའི་ཕྱིར། སྐུ་ཉིད་མངོན་སུམ་དུ་ཕེབས་ནས་ས་གནས་འདི་ལ་མཆོད་པ་ཕུལ་ལོ།། ⟨བརྟག⟩

## Passage II · The stone horse (?) and the stone pillar  (Hultzsch B)

### Prakrit (lines 3–4; Hultzsch 1925 p. 164)

#### Devanāgarī row (as printed)
> 3 **(B)** सिला विगडभी चा कालापित सिलाथभे च उसपापिते
> 4 हिद भगवं जाते ति …

#### Roman transliteration
> 3 **(B)** silā vigaḍabhī chā kālāpita silā-thabhe cha usapāpite
> 4 hida **Bhagavaṁ** jāte ti …

### English (Hultzsch 1925 p. 164)
> **(B)** (He) both caused to be made a stone bearing a horse (?)³ and caused a stone pillar to be set up, (in order to show) that **the Blessed one** was born here.⁴

³ p. 164 n. 3: "This is Charpentier's explanation of the obscure and much-discussed word *vigaḍabhī*; see IA, 43. 19 f. The syllable *-bhī* might certainly represent Skt. *-bhṛit*; but, that *vigaḍa* means 'a horse', remains to be proved by more substantial evidence. Sir R. Bhandarkar (JBBRAS, 20. 366, n. 14) and Fleet (JRAS, 1908. 477, 823) joined *bhīchā* into one word, and explained it by Skt. \**bhittikā*, 'a wall'. As, according to Pischel's *Grammatik*, §§ 49, 219, *vigaḍa* is an Ardhamāgadhī form of Skt. *vikṛita*, *silā-vigaḍa bhīchā* might mean 'a (brick) wall decorated with stone'. But, as Charpentier (IA, 43. 17) remarks, the change of \**bhittikā* to *bhīchā* 'is wholly improbable from a linguistic point of view, as consonants were not dropped to such an extent at that early stage of Prākṛit dialects.'"

⁴ p. 164 n. 4: "With the words *hida Bhagavaṁ jāte* cf. *Divyāvadāna*, p. 389: '*asmin mahārāja pradēśē Bhagavāñ jātaḥ.*'"

### 现代汉语 (Sarasvatī machine draft ⟨བརྟག⟩)
> （B）**又**令作**载马之石**（？）并令建立石柱，以表示**世尊**诞生于此。

### བོད་ཡིག (Sarasvatī machine draft ⟨བརྟག⟩)
> (B) རྟ་ཁུར་བའི་རྡོ་དང་རྡོ་རིང་གཉིས་ཀ་བཞེངས་སུ་བཅུག་སྟེ། བཅོམ་ལྡན་འདས་འདིར་སྐུ་བལྟམས་པར་སྟོན་ནོ།། ⟨བརྟག⟩

## Passage III · Lumbinī made tax-free, paying only an eighth share  (Hultzsch C)

### Prakrit (lines 4–5; Hultzsch 1925 p. 164)

#### Devanāgarī row (as printed)
> 4 … **(C)** लुंमिनिगामे उबलिके कटे
> 5 अठभागिये च

#### Roman transliteration
> 4 … **(C) Luṁmini-gāme** ubalike kaṭe
> 5 aṭha-bhāgiye cha

### English (Hultzsch 1925 p. 165)
> **(C)** (He) made **the village of Luṁmini**¹ free of taxes,² and paying (only) an eighth share (of the produce).³

¹ p. 165 n. 1: "In the *Nidānakathā* (*Jātaka*, vol. I, pp. 52 and 54), the site of the Buddha's birth is called Lumbinī-vana. For other forms of the name see Charpentier in IA, 43. 18. The word Luṁmini survives in the modern designation Rummindēī; see V. A. Smith in IA, 34. 1."

² p. 165 n. 2: "For *ubalika* = Skt. \**udbalika* see Barth, *Journal des Savants*, 1897, p. 73, n. 2; Bühler, EI, 5. 5; Fleet, JRAS, 1908. 478 f.; Sir Charles Lyall, id. 850 f.; and Thomas (id. 1909. 466 f.), who quotes the similar term *uchchhulka* from the *Kauṭilīya*."

³ p. 165 n. 3: "The *Divyāvadāna* (p. 390) reports that Aśōka spent at Lumbinī-vana 100,000 (gold pieces). In accordance with this legend, Barth (loc. cit.), Bühler (EI, 5. 5), and Neumann (ZDMG, 68. 721 f.) took *aṭha* = Skt. *artha* and translated *aṭha-bhāgiya* by 'partaking of riches'. Fleet (JRAS, 1908. 479 f.) explained *aṭha-bhāga* by Skt. *ashṭa-bhāga*, 'an eighth share', which the king is permitted by Manu (VII, 130) to levy on grains. In the *Kauṭilīya* (p. 116, l. 2 from bottom) *chaturtha-pañcha-bhāgika* is actually used with the meaning 'paying a fourth or a fifth share (of the produce)'. I therefore agree with Thomas (JRAS, 1914. 391 f.) in believing that *-bhāgiya* must mean 'paying a share', and not, as Fleet thought, 'entitled to a share'. In the case of the village of Luṁmini, bureaucracy prevailed against charity. For the affix *-iya* (= Skt. *-ika*) see above, p. 124, n. 4."

### 现代汉语 (Sarasvatī machine draft ⟨བརྟག⟩)
> （C）又将**蓝毗尼村**宣定为免税之地，仅令缴纳（物产之）八分之一。

### བོད་ཡིག (Sarasvatī machine draft ⟨བརྟག⟩)
> (C) **ལུམྦི་ནཱི་གྲོང་** ཁྲལ་ལས་གྲོལ་བར་མཛད་ཅིང་། (ལོ་ཐོག་གི་)ཆ་བརྒྱད་ཀྱི་ཆ་གཅིག་ཁོ་ན་སྤྲོད་པར་མཛད་དོ།། ⟨བརྟག⟩

## Note on the plate (n339)

The photographic plate labelled "RUMMINDEI PILLAR-INSCRIPTION · PAGE 164 · Scale One-Fourth" (IA leaf n339) carries the 5-line Brāhmī estampage, with line numbers `2` and `4` in the margins. A separate Nigālī Sāgar estampage sits below it on the same plate, partly broken away at the upper right and lower left (large blank areas where the stone surface is lost). The plate was **opened and read for layout**, not transcribed; the Brāhmī itself is not re-collated here against Hultzsch's Devanāgarī row. The pillar still stands *in situ* at the Rummindeī site (Rupandehī district, Nepal), rediscovered by Fuhrer in 1896; a modern epigraphic re-collation against the stone remains outside this file's scope.

**Open questions** (for future reviewers):

- Section-C word division: Hultzsch sets `Luṁmini-gāme` with a hyphen in Roman but `लुंमिनिगामे` solid in Devanāgarī. The pillar's Brāhmī spacing to be rechecked.
- The *vigaḍabhī* crux (n. 3) remains the most-discussed word in the inscription. Hultzsch's Charpentier-based reading "a stone bearing a horse (?)" is marked with his own `?`; the Bhandarkar/Fleet \**bhittikā* ("a wall") and the Pischel-grammatical "a (brick) wall decorated with stone" alternatives are also preserved in his footnote. **No modern consensus.**
- The joining of `hida Budhe jāte Sakyamunī ti` to section A (Hultzsch's choice) vs section B (Fleet's) depends on interpretation; Hultzsch's parse is kept here.
- Whether `vīsati-vasa-abhisitena` ("20 years after consecration") corresponds to the same regnal year as Rock Edict VIII's pilgrimage statement, or to a distinct later pilgrimage, remains open.
- Natural continuation for the India branch: **Nigālī Sāgar pillar-inscription** (same scan, pp. 165–166, leaves n340/n341) — Aśoka's doubling of the Buddha Konāgamana's stūpa in his 14th regnal year; Candidate B of this week's proposal.
