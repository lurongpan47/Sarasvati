# Aśoka's Letter to the Saṅgha of Magadha · 阿育王致摩揭陀僧伽书 · མྱ་ངན་མེད་ཀྱིས་མ་ག་དྷཱའི་དགེ་འདུན་ལ་ཕུལ་བའི་འཕྲིན་ཡིག

*Calcutta-Bairāṭ rock-inscription (the "Bhābrū edict") · 4-language reading · India branch, third sample — built scan-first*

**Sources**: Prakrit (Calcutta-Bairāṭ block, Roman transliteration rows in E. Hultzsch, *Inscriptions of Asoka*, New Edition = *Corpus Inscriptionum Indicarum* vol. I, Oxford 1925, **pp. 172–173**, lines 1–8, with his notes p. 172 nn. 6–11 and p. 173 nn. 1–11; public domain) · English (Hultzsch 1925, **pp. 173–174**, "Translation", with his notes p. 173 nn. 12–19 and p. 174 n. 1; public domain) · 现代汉语 (Sarasvatī machine draft from the Prakrit + Hultzsch English ⟨བརྟག⟩) · བོད་ཡིག (Sarasvatī machine draft ⟨བརྟག⟩).

**Scan used**: Internet Archive item `InscriptionsOfAsoka.NewEditionByE.Hultzsch` (`HultzschCorpusAsokaSearchable_jp2.zip`), full-size leaves **n349 = p. 172**, **n352 = p. 173**, **n353 = p. 174** (5909 × 8117 each, BookReader endpoint `scale=1`), and the estampage plate **n351** ("CALCUTTA-BAIRAT ROCK-INSCRIPTION · PAGE 173 · Scale One-Fourth", 8 lines numbered 2/4/6/8 in the margins), all read on 2026-09-28. Page numbers were **read from the printed running heads** — from p. 143 onward the leaf-to-page offset is no longer the 136 that held for pp. 20–45, because the plates are interleaved (n308 = p. 143, n344 = p. 168, n349 = p. 172). Transliteration rows were read in 8 half-line strips at 1.5–1.6×; every footnote numeral was re-read at 1.3–1.5× where the OCR layer disagreed (p. 172 n. 8 → `727`, not `737`; p. 173 n. 12 → `IA, 21. 232`; n. 17 → `IA, 5. 257`, not `357`). The Devanāgarī rows (p. 172 ll. 1–8) were **not** transcribed. The **⟨བརྟག⟩ columns are not attested** by anything.

**Branch**: India · 印度源流 — third sample after Major Rock Edicts XII and XIII. This is the earliest dated document anywhere that names Buddhist **texts** (seven *dhaṁma-paliyāyāni*) and asks a community to recite and reflect on them: the hinge between the India branch and the Pāli branch, and the epigraphic root of everything else in this archive.

## Reading conventions

- Hultzsch's transliteration conventions are kept: *ch* = c, *chh* = ch, *ṁ* = anusvāra, square brackets `[ ]` = akṣaras damaged or supplied, superscript numerals = his notes. His **heavy type** (Clarendon) is kept in bold where it falls (the names *Pr[i]yadas[i]*, *l[ā]jā Māgadhe*, *Budhasi*, *Budhe[na]*, *Budhena*); we do not interpret it.
- Line numbers **1–8** are the rock's lines as Hultzsch prints them; sentence letters **(A)–(G)** are his. Run-over words in his layout are joined here (l. 4 `dha]ṁma-paliyāyāni`, l. 5–6 `Lāghulo-vāde`).
- Notes are quoted verbatim, then a raised dash `—` introduces any remark of ours (always so marked).

## Passage I · Greeting and confession of faith  (Hultzsch A–B)

### Prakrit (lines 1–2; Hultzsch 1925 p. 172)
> 1 (A) **Pr[i]yadas[i]**⁶ **l[ā]jā Māgadhe**⁷ saṁghaṁ abhivāde[tū]naṁ⁸ āhā ap[ā]bādhataṁ cha phāsu-vihālataṁ chā
> 2 (B) vidite v[e] bhaṁte āvatake h[a]mā **Budhasi** dhaṁmasi saṁghasī ti gālave⁹ chaṁ prasāde¹⁰ cha

⁶ p. 172 n. 6: "*Piya°* Senart." · ⁷ n. 7: "*Māgadhaṁ* Senart; but the *e* of *dhe* is quite distinct, and what has been taken for an Anusvāra is in reality a horizontal dash." · ⁸ n. 8: "*abhivādanaṁ* Senart; but see JRAS, 1909. 727." · ⁹ n. 9: "*galave* Senart." · ¹⁰ n. 10: "*pasāde* Senart."

### English (Hultzsch 1925 p. 173)
> (A) The Māgadha king Priyadarśin,¹² having saluted the *Saṁgha*,¹³ hopes they are both well and comfortable.¹⁴
> (B) It is known to you, Sirs, how great is my reverence and faith in the Buddha, the *Dharma*,¹⁵ (and) the *Saṁgha*.¹⁶

¹² p. 173 n. 12: "Cf. e.g. *rājā Māgadho Seniyo Bimbisāro* in the *Vinaya-piṭaka* (passim); *rājā Māgadho Ajātasattu* at the beginning of the *Mahāparinibbāna-sutta* (JRAS, 1875. 49); and *rājā Pasenaji Kosalo* in a Bharaut inscription (IA, 21. 232, No. 58)." · ¹³ n. 13: "i. e. the Buddhist clergy." · ¹⁴ n. 14: "Literally: 'pronounces both (their) being in good health and (their) being (in the enjoyment) of pleasant life'. Cf. at the beginning of the *Mahāparinibbāna-sutta*: '*appābādhaṁ appātaṅkaṁ lahuṭṭhānaṁ balaṁ phāsu-vihāraṁ puchchha*', and see Neumann's translations of the *Majjhima-nikāya* (2. 617, note) and *Dīgha-nikāya* (2. 216, n. 3)." · ¹⁵ n. 15: "i. e. the Buddhist doctrine." · ¹⁶ n. 16: "These are the so-called 'three jewels'; see Childers' *Pāli Dictionary*, s. v. *ratanaṁ*."

### 现代汉语 (Sarasvatī machine draft ⟨བརྟག⟩)
> （A）摩揭陀王喜见，敬礼僧伽，愿众无病，安乐而住。
> （B）诸大德，我于佛、法、僧之恭敬与净信何等深厚，汝等所知。

### བོད་ཡིག (Sarasvatī machine draft ⟨བརྟག⟩)
> (A) མ་ག་དྷཱའི་རྒྱལ་པོ་མཛེས་མཐོང་གིས་དགེ་འདུན་ལ་ཕྱག་འཚལ་ནས། ནད་མེད་པ་དང་བདེ་བར་གནས་པར་སྨོན་ཏོ།།
> (B) བཙུན་པ་དག། བདག་སངས་རྒྱས་དང་ཆོས་དང་དགེ་འདུན་ལ་གུས་པ་དང་དད་པ་ཇི་ཙམ་ཡོད་པ་ནི་ཁྱེད་ཅག་གིས་མཁྱེན་ཏོ།། ⟨བརྟག⟩

## Passage II · Whatever the Buddha spoke is well spoken; the king's duty to speak  (Hultzsch C–D)

### Prakrit (lines 2–4; Hultzsch 1925 pp. 172–173)
> 2 … (C) e kechi¹¹ bhaṁte
> 3 bhagavatā **Budhe[na]** bhāsite sarve¹ se subhāsite vā (D) e chu kho bhaṁte hamiyāye diseyā hevaṁ sadhaṁme
> 4 chil[a-ṭhi]tīke hosatī ti alahāmi hakaṁ ta[ṁ] v[ā]tave² …

¹¹ p. 172 n. 11: "*keṁchi* Senart." · ¹ p. 173 n. 1: "*save* Senart." · ² n. 2: "*vatave* Senart."

### English (Hultzsch 1925 p. 173)
> (C) Whatsoever, Sirs, has been spoken by the blessed Buddha, all that is quite well spoken.
> (D) But, Sirs, what would indeed appear to me¹⁷ (to be referred to by the words of the scripture): 'thus the true *Dharma* will be of long duration',¹⁸ that I feel bound to declare.

¹⁷ p. 173 n. 17: "Kern (IA, 5. 257) explained *diseyā* as the optative of the Pāli *dissati* = Skt. *dṛiśyatē*; cf. JRAS, 1909. 728." · ¹⁸ n. 18: "As suggested by the late Professor Hardy (JRAS, 1901. 314), these words seem to contain a quotation from the Buddhist scriptures."

### 现代汉语 (Sarasvatī machine draft ⟨བརྟག⟩)
> （C）诸大德，凡世尊佛所说，一切皆是善说。
> （D）然诸大德，（圣教所云）"如是正法得以久住"者，我以为其义何在，我自觉应当宣说。

### བོད་ཡིག (Sarasvatī machine draft ⟨བརྟག⟩)
> (C) བཙུན་པ་དག། བཅོམ་ལྡན་འདས་སངས་རྒྱས་ཀྱིས་ཅི་གསུངས་པ་ཐམས་ཅད་ནི་ལེགས་པར་གསུངས་པ་ཁོ་ན་ཡིན།
> (D) འོན་ཀྱང་བཙུན་པ་དག། "འདི་ལྟར་དམ་པའི་ཆོས་ཡུན་རིང་དུ་གནས་པར་འགྱུར" ཅེས་པའི་(གསུང་རབ་ཀྱི་ཚིག་དེའི་)དོན་བདག་ལ་ཇི་ལྟར་སྣང་བ་དེ་བརྗོད་པར་འོས་སོ་སྙམ་དུ་བདག་སེམས་སོ།། ⟨བརྟག⟩

## Passage III · The seven expositions of the Dharma  (Hultzsch E)

### Prakrit (lines 4–7; Hultzsch 1925 p. 173)
> 4 … (E) imāni bhaṁt[e dha]ṁma-paliyāyāni Vinaya-samukase
> 5 Aliya-vasāṇi³ Anāgata-bhayāni Muni-gāthā Moneya-sūte Upatisa-pasine e chā Lāghulo-
> 6 vāde musā-vādaṁ adhigichya⁴ bhagavatā **Budhena** bhāsite etāni⁵ bhaṁte dhaṁma-paliyāyāni ichhāmi
> 7 kiṁti bahuke bhikhu-[p]āye chā bhikhuniye⁶ ch[ā] abhikhinaṁ sun[e]yu⁷ chā upadhāl[a]yeyū⁸ chā

³ p. 173 n. 3: "*-vasāni* Senart." · ⁴ n. 4: "This word is quite distinct, and the reading *adhigiḍhya* proposed by Michelson (IF, 27. 194 f.) is impossible." · ⁵ n. 5: "*etāna* Senart." · ⁶ n. 6: "*bhakhuniye* Senart." · ⁷ n. 7: "*sunayu* Senart." · ⁸ n. 8: "*upādhāleyeyu* Senart." — The strip read of l. 5 shows a subscript dot under the *n* of *Aliya-vasāṇi* (retroflex ṇ) while Hultzsch's n. 3 cites Senart with dental *-vasāni*; a reviewer with the printed volume should confirm the ṇ.

### English (Hultzsch 1925 pp. 173–174)
> (E) The following expositions of the *Dharma*, Sirs, (viz.) (1) the *Vinaya-samukasa*, (2) the *Aliya-vasas*,¹⁹ (3) the *Anāgata-bhayas*, (4) the *Muni-gāthās*, (5) the *Moneya-sūta*, (6) the *Upatisa-pasina*, and (7) the *Lāghulovāda* which was spoken by the blessed Buddha concerning falsehood,¹ — I desire, Sirs, that many groups of monks and (many) nuns may repeatedly listen to these expositions of the *Dharma*, and may reflect (on them).

¹⁹ p. 173 n. 19: "*vasa* is a defective spelling for *vaṁsa*; see IA, 41. 39." · ¹ p. 174 n. 1: "For identifications of these texts see Oldenberg, *Vinaya-piṭaka*, Introduction, p. xl, n. 1; Sylvain Lévi, JA (9), 7. 475 ff.; Neumann, VOJ, 11. 159 f.; Rhys Davids, JRAS, 1898. 639 f.; Oldenberg, ZDMG, 52. 634 ff.; Bloch, id., 63. 325 ff. As the late lamented Mr. V. A. Smith kindly informed me, No. 1, *Vinaya-samukasa*, is identified by A. J. Edmunds (*Buddhist Bibliography*, San Francisco, 1904) with Buddha's First Sermon. According to Dharmananda Kosambi (IA, 41. 40), Nos. 2 and 3 occur in the *Aṅguttara-nikāya*, Nos. 4–6 in the *Sutta-nipāta*, and No. 7 in the *Majjhima-nikāya*." — The "p. xl" is read from the clipped top line of the right-hand strip (lower halves of the glyphs only); the other digits were read whole.

### 现代汉语 (Sarasvatī machine draft ⟨བརྟག⟩)
> （E）诸大德，此等法教——（一）《律最胜》（Vinaya-samukasa），（二）《圣种》（Aliya-vasa），（三）《未来诸怖畏》（Anāgata-bhaya），（四）《牟尼偈》（Muni-gāthā），（五）《寂默行经》（Moneya-sūta），（六）《优波提舍所问》（Upatisa-pasina），及（七）世尊佛就妄语所说之《罗睺罗教诫》（Lāghulovāda）——诸大德，我愿众多比丘众及诸比丘尼数数听闻此等法教，并思惟之。

### བོད་ཡིག (Sarasvatī machine draft ⟨བརྟག⟩)
> (E) བཙུན་པ་དག། ཆོས་ཀྱི་གཞུང་འདི་དག་སྟེ། (༡) འདུལ་བ་མཆོག་ཏུ་བསྟན་པ་དང་། (༢) འཕགས་པའི་རིགས་དག་དང་། (༣) མ་འོངས་པའི་འཇིགས་པ་དག་དང་། (༤) ཐུབ་པའི་ཚིགས་སུ་བཅད་པ་དག་དང་། (༥) ཐུབ་པའི་སྤྱོད་པའི་མདོ་དང་། (༦) ཉེ་རྒྱལ་གྱིས་ཞུས་པ་དང་། (༧) བཅོམ་ལྡན་འདས་སངས་རྒྱས་ཀྱིས་བརྫུན་དུ་སྨྲ་བའི་སྐོར་ལ་གསུངས་པའི་སྒྲ་གཅན་ཟིན་ལ་གདམས་པ་སྟེ། བཙུན་པ་དག། དགེ་སློང་གི་ཚོགས་མང་པོ་དང་དགེ་སློང་མ་མང་པོ་དག་ཆོས་ཀྱི་གཞུང་འདི་དག་ཡང་དང་ཡང་དུ་ཉན་ཅིང་སེམས་པར་བདག་འདོད་དོ།། ⟨བརྟག⟩

## Passage IV · Laity likewise; the king's intention  (Hultzsch F–G)

### Prakrit (line 8; Hultzsch 1925 p. 173)
> 8 (F) hevaṁmevā upāsakā chā upāsikā chā (G) eteni⁹ bhaṁte imaṁ likhā[pa]yāmi abhipretaṁ¹⁰ me jānaṁtū¹¹ ti

⁹ p. 173 n. 9: "Read *etenā*." · ¹⁰ n. 10: "*abhihetaṁ* Senart; but the horizontal stroke attached to *pa* is probably intended for *r*. Cf. *aprakaraṇamhi* in the Girnār edict XII, l. 3." — the same akṣara discussed in our REXII file, Passage II n. 4. · ¹¹ n. 11: "*ma jānaṁta* Senart."

### English (Hultzsch 1925 p. 174)
> (F) In the same way both laymen and laywomen (should act).
> (G) For the following (purpose), Sirs, am I causing this to be written, (viz.) in order that they may know my intention.

### 现代汉语 (Sarasvatī machine draft ⟨བརྟག⟩)
> （F）优婆塞、优婆夷亦当如是。
> （G）诸大德，我为此故令刻此文：愿彼等知我本意。

### བོད་ཡིག (Sarasvatī machine draft ⟨བརྟག⟩)
> (F) དེ་བཞིན་དུ་དགེ་བསྙེན་ཕོ་མོ་དག་གིས་ཀྱང་བྱའོ།།
> (G) བཙུན་པ་དག། དེ་དག་གིས་བདག་གི་བསམ་པ་ཤེས་པར་བྱ་བའི་ཕྱིར་ན་བདག་གིས་འདི་བྲིར་བཅུག་གོ།། ⟨བརྟག⟩

**Historical note (editorial, not from the scan).** Hultzsch's Introduction (p. xxv per the OCR layer, not opened at full size) records the block's history: discovered in 1840 by Captain Burt "upon a hill lying adjacent to" Bairāṭ (JASB, 9. 616), a grey granite block now preserved by the Asiatic Society of Bengal in Calcutta — hence his name for it, which he prefers to the usual "Bhābrū edict" because the town is called Bhābrū, not Bhābra, and lies twelve miles from the find-spot; Cunningham knew the hill as *Bījak-Pahāṛ*, "inscription hill". Earlier editions: Burnouf, Kern, Wilson (from Burt's copy), Senart (*Inscriptions de Piyadasi* 2. 197 ff.; revised from Burgess's estampage in IA, 20. 165 ff.). Unlike the Major Rock Edicts, this text is addressed not to the king's subjects but to the Saṅgha itself; the king styles himself *lājā Māgadhe*, "the Magadha king", nowhere else in his corpus. The seven titles have been matched to Pāli texts since Oldenberg (1879) — e.g. *Muni-gāthā* ≈ Sn 1.12 *Munisutta*, *Moneya-sūte* ≈ Sn 3.11 *Nālakasutta* or AN 3.120, *Upatisa-pasine* ≈ Sn 4.16 *Sāriputtasutta*, *Lāghulovāde* on falsehood ≈ MN 61 *Ambalaṭṭhikārāhulovāda*, *Aliya-vasāṇi* ≈ AN 10.20 *Ariyavāsa*, *Anāgata-bhayāni* ≈ AN 5.77–80 — but none is settled (Hultzsch p. 174 n. 1 gives the state of the question in 1925); we print the titles untranslated in the Prakrit and English columns and gloss them only in the ⟨བརྟག⟩ columns, where a reader may correct them.

**ཕྱི་ལོའི་སྔོན་གྱི་དུས་རབས་གསུམ་པའི་རྒྱལ་པོ་ཞིག་གིས་བརྗོད་པ། "ཆོས་ཀྱི་གཞུང་འདི་དག་ཡང་དང་ཡང་དུ་ཉོན་ཅིག། སེམས་ཅིག།" — དེ་ནི་མིང་ཅན་གྱི་གསུང་རབ་ལ་བརྟེན་པའི་ཐོག་མའི་བཀའ་ཡིན་ནོ།།**
