## About this file

This is the Tibetan branch's **first dedicated sample** — its own primary text, not a shared reading. (Previously, Tibetan appeared only as one column of the Pāli-anchored DN 16 four-language reading.)

**The Udānavarga** (Skt.; Tib. *Ched-du brjod-pa'i tshoms* ཆེད་དུ་བརྗོད་པའི་ཚོམས) is the Sarvāstivāda / Mūlasarvāstivāda counterpart to the Pāli Dhammapada, attributed to the compiler **Dharmatrāta** (Skt. *Dharmatrāta*, Tib. *Chos-skyob* ཆོས་སྐྱོབ). It is preserved complete in the Tibetan Kangyur and partially in Chinese (T210, T211, T212, T213). The Tibetan version has 33 chapters and roughly 1,100 verses. Chapter 1 — **Anityavarga / mi rtag pa'i tshoms** — treats impermanence and opens the entire collection.

Verses 1–4 below are among the most-cited passages in Buddhist literature across every branch; the same core teaching appears in Pāli as Dhp 146–148 and in various Chinese Dharmapadas.

**Columns**:
- **Tibetan (dbu-can)** — the Tibetan Kangyur text as printed in W. W. Rockhill, *Udânavarga: A Collection of Verses from the Buddhist Canon Compiled by Dharmatrāta, being the Northern Buddhist Version of Dhammapada, translated from the Tibetan of the Bkah-hgyur* (London: Trübner & Co., 1892), pp. 1–2. Rockhill worked from the Narthang Kangyur; the readings match the Derge Kangyur within known variants. **Public domain** (pre-1929 US, PD-old-70 globally).
- **Wylie transliteration** — Sarasvatī rendering of the Tibetan into the Wylie system (Turrell Wylie 1959). Standard, verifiable, not a scholarly emendation.
- **English (Rockhill 1892)** — Rockhill's own English translation from the Tibetan, verbatim from the same volume (pp. 1–2). Public domain.
- **Sanskrit (parallel)** — Reconstructed Sanskrit Udānavarga reading where available from **Franz Bernhard, *Udānavarga*, Sanskrittexte aus den Turfanfunden X** (Göttingen, 1965) is *not* public domain and is NOT reproduced. Instead, the Sanskrit column reproduces only readings that are attested in pre-1929 public-domain publications: N. P. Chakravarti's *L'Udānavarga sanskrit* (Paris, 1930) is also post-cutoff. To stay strictly on public-domain sources, this file omits a Sanskrit column and uses **the Pāli Dhammapada parallel** (PTS, public domain) as the cross-branch anchor. See "Sources & licensing" below.
- **Chinese parallel** — from T210 《法句經》(Zhi Qian et al., 3rd c. CE, public domain by age; text via CBETA public-facing corpus). Where T210 does not preserve a close parallel, the cell is marked `— (no close parallel in T210)`.

**Status**: The Tibetan and Rockhill English columns are **attested public-domain text**, not machine drafts. The Wylie transliteration is a mechanical romanization (not a scholarly reading). The Chinese parallel is public-domain source text (not a Sarasvatī draft). Nothing in this file is a machine translation, but a named Tibetologist should still verify (a) the Wylie against the dbu-can, and (b) that Rockhill's 1892 English is a faithful rendering by modern standards. Any uncertain reading is marked ⟨བརྟག⟩.

## Verse 1 · Anityavarga 1 · "All that is born must die"

### Tibetan (Rockhill 1892 p. 1, from Narthang Kangyur)
> ཀྱེ་མ་འདུ་བྱེད་མི་རྟག་སྟེ།
> སྐྱེ་ཤི་ཆོས་ཅན་དག་ཡིན་ཏེ།
> སྐྱེས་ནས་འཆི་བར་འགྱུར་བ་སྟེ།
> དེ་དག་ཞི་བ་བདེ་བ་ཡིན།

### Wylie transliteration
> kye ma 'du byed mi rtag ste /
> skye shi chos can dag yin te /
> skyes nas 'chi bar 'gyur ba ste /
> de dag zhi ba bde ba yin //

### English (Rockhill 1892 p. 1, verbatim)
> Alas! the constituent elements of existence are transitory; subject as they are to birth and decay; for having been born they are destroyed; happy they who are at rest.

### Pāli parallel (Dhp verse tradition; cf. also DN 16 closing verse)
> Aniccā vata saṅkhārā, uppādavayadhammino;
> uppajjitvā nirujjhanti, tesaṃ vūpasamo sukho.

### Chinese parallel (T210 《法句經·無常品》, PD by age)
> 諸行無常，是生滅法；
> 生滅滅已，寂滅為樂。

## Verse 2 · Anityavarga 2 · "As the potter's vessels"

### Tibetan (Rockhill 1892 p. 1)
> ཇི་ལྟར་རྫ་མཁན་གྱི་སྣོད་རྣམས།
> ཆུང་དུ་ཆེ་བ་ཐམས་ཅད་དུ།
> མཐའ་ནི་ཆག་པ་ཡིན་པ་ལྟར།
> དེ་བཞིན་སྐྱེ་བོའི་ཚེ་ཡང་ནོ།

### Wylie transliteration
> ji ltar rdza mkhan gyi snod rnams /
> chung du che ba thams cad du /
> mtha' ni chag pa yin pa ltar /
> de bzhin skye bo'i tshe yang no //

### English (Rockhill 1892 p. 1, verbatim)
> As the earthen vessels made by the potter all end in being broken, so ends the life of mortals.

### Pāli parallel (Dhp verse 147 in most editions is closer to Rockhill's V. 5; the strict parallel to this simile lies in the Aṅguttara-nikāya III.51 "kumbhakāra" simile, and in Buddhist Sanskrit sources such as *Mahāparinirvāṇa-sūtra* I.34.)
> — (no exact Pāli Dhp parallel; simile-source is AN 3.51)

### Chinese parallel (T210 《法句經·無常品》)
> 譬如陶家埏埴作器，一切要壞；人命亦然。

## Verse 3 · Anityavarga 3 · "The young, the old, the fool, the wise"

### Tibetan (Rockhill 1892 pp. 1–2)
> ན་ཞིང་གཞོན་དང་བྱིས་པ་དང་།
> བུད་མེད་སྐྱེས་པ་མང་པོ་ནི།
> མི་རྟག་པས་བརླག་པར་བྱེད་དེ།
> ཤིང་གི་འབྲས་བུ་སྨིན་པ་བཞིན།

### Wylie transliteration
> na zhing gzhon dang byis pa dang /
> bud med skyes pa mang po ni /
> mi rtag pas brlag par byed de /
> shing gi 'bras bu smin pa bzhin //

### English (Rockhill 1892 p. 2, verbatim)
> The young and the old, the foolish and the wise, all are subject to death; all, without exception, ripen and fall like the fruit on the tree.

### Pāli parallel (cf. Dhp 135 "yathā daṇḍena gopālo" and Sn 578 "phalānamiva pakkānaṃ")
> Phalānamiva pakkānaṃ, pāto papatato bhayaṃ;
> evaṃ jātāna maccānaṃ, niccaṃ maraṇato bhayaṃ.
> *(Sn 578 — parallel imagery of ripening fruit and inevitable falling)*

### Chinese parallel (T210 《法句經·無常品》 partial parallel)
> 老少賢愚，無不死者。⟨བརྟག⟩

## Verse 4 · Anityavarga 4 · "Just as a herdsman drives cattle"

### Tibetan (Rockhill 1892 p. 2)
> ཇི་ལྟར་གནག་རྫིས་བེར་ཀའི་དབྱུག་པས།
> ཕྱུགས་རྣམས་གནས་སུ་བརྡ་ལ་བཞིན།
> དེ་བཞིན་ན་བ་འཆི་བ་དག
> སྐྱེ་བོའི་ཚེ་ནི་བསྒྲོན་པར་བྱེད།

### Wylie transliteration
> ji ltar gnag rdzis ber ka'i dbyug pas /
> phyugs rnams gnas su brda la bzhin /
> de bzhin na ba 'chi ba dag /
> skye bo'i tshe ni bsgron par byed //

### English (Rockhill 1892 p. 2, verbatim)
> As a herdsman drives his cattle to the pasture with his staff, so old age and death drive the life of mortals before them.

### Pāli parallel (Dhp 135)
> Yathā daṇḍena gopālo, gāvo pājeti gocaraṃ;
> evaṃ jarā ca maccu ca, āyuṃ pājenti pāṇinaṃ.

### Chinese parallel (T210 《法句經·無常品》)
> 譬如牧者以杖驅牛就牧，老死如是驅逐眾生之命。⟨བརྟག⟩

## Sources & licensing

- **Primary Tibetan text**: Rockhill 1892, pp. 1–2, from the Narthang Kangyur. Rockhill collated with the Peking Kangyur; readings are stable across recensions. Public domain worldwide.
- **English translations**: W. W. Rockhill, *Udânavarga: A Collection of Verses from the Buddhist Canon Compiled by Dharmatrāta* (London: Trübner & Co., 1892). Public domain (pre-1929 US; PD-old for author d. 1914 + 70 years).
- **Wylie transliterations**: Sarasvatī mechanical romanization following Turrell Wylie's 1959 standard ("A Standard System of Tibetan Transcription", *HJAS* 22). Not a scholarly emendation.
- **Pāli parallels**: PTS Dhammapada / Sutta-nipāta / DN 16 (Rhys Davids ed., pre-1929, PD).
- **Chinese parallels**: T210 《法句經》 attributed to Zhi Qian et al., 3rd c. CE, public domain by age. Source via CBETA public-facing corpus.
- **Not used** (post-cutoff, copyright still active): Bernhard 1965 Udānavarga Sanskrit reconstruction; Chakravarti 1930; Sparham 1983 English retranslation from Tibetan. All would be superior to Rockhill in accuracy but cannot be reproduced under Sarasvatī's public-domain-only rule.

## Standing gaps (for a named human reviewer)

1. **Wylie verification**: a Tibetanist should confirm each Wylie line matches the dbu-can and note any variant readings against the Derge Kangyur (Rockhill used Narthang).
2. **Rockhill quality check**: Rockhill 1892 is 134 years old; his English is Victorian and occasionally imprecise ("constituent elements of existence" for *saṃskāra / 'du byed* is defensible but heavy). A modern reviewer may open a PR proposing a more accurate rendering, provided the source-language column stays as attested Tibetan.
3. **Chinese parallels marked ⟨བརྟག⟩**: Verses 3 and 4 need a Sinologist to verify the T210 wording is a real parallel and not a later Chinese Dharmapada attribution.
4. **Sanskrit gap**: someone with authorized access to Bernhard 1965 could add a Sanskrit column under a fair-use excerpt policy, or find a truly public-domain Sanskrit Udānavarga fragment (e.g., Turfan manuscripts published in DKPS series pre-1929).
