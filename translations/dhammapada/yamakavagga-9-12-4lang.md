## Verse 9 · Anikkasāvo kāsāvaṁ … na so kāsāvaṁ arhati

### Pāli (Fausbøll 1900, p. 4 [leaf 0021, printed "4"]; verse 9)
> Anikkasāvo kāsāvaṁ yo vatthaṁ paridahessati¹  
> apeto damasaccena na so kāsāvaṁ arhati².

*No punctuation after* paridahessati¹ *(8×). The final* ṁ *of both* kāsāvaṁ *carries the dot (3×, 8×). The mark between* A *and* n *of* Anikkasāvo *is a thin, high, slanted tick lighter than the type (8×; at 3× it was read as a possible apostrophe) — an accident, not reproduced.*

Fausbøll, *Variants and Notes*, V. 9 (foot of leaf 0021, line 2, running into line 3): ¹ Bʳ -dahissati; read paridhassati? ² MSS. & Editions arahati.

*The conjecture* paridhassati? *is read identically at 2.5× and 8×. After* Bʳ *there is a small fleck at the baseline (8× "a low period"; 14×: a third the size of the periods after* V *and* 9*, lighter) — not a printed stop, not reproduced. The line ends after* Editions *and* arahati. *opens line 3 (8×).*

### English (Müller 1881, SBE X, p. 5 [tif 0065]; verse 9)
> 9. He who wishes to put on the yellow dress without having cleansed himself from sin, who disregards also temperance and truth, is unworthy of the yellow dress.

Müller, note 9 (p. 5, six lines, | = the page turn, then p. 6, thirteen lines): 9. The dark yellow dress, the Kâsâva or Kâshâya, is the distinctive garment of the Buddhist priests. See Vish*n*u-sûtra LXIII, 36. The play on the words anikkasâvo kâsâvam, or in Sanskrit anishkashâya*h* kâshâyam, cannot be rendered in English. Kashâya means ' impurity,' nish-kashâya, ' free from impurity,' anish-kashâya, ' not free from impurity,' while kâshâya is the name of the yellowish | Buddhist garment. The pun is evidently a favourite one, for, as Fausböll shows, it occurs also in the Mahâbhârata, XII, 568 :
Anishkashâye kâshâyam îhârtham iti viddhi tam,
Dharmadhva*g*ânâ*m* mu*nd*ânâ*m* v*ri*ttyartham iti me mati*h*.
' Know that this yellow-coloured garment on a man who is not free from impurity, serves only for the purpose of cupidity ; my opinion is, that it is meant to supply the means of living to those shavelings, who carry their virtue or the dharma like a flag.'
(I read v*ri*ttyartham, according to the Bombay edition, instead of k*ri*târtham, the reading of the Calcutta edition.)
On the exact colour of the dress, see Bishop Bigandet, The Life or Legend of Gaudama, the Budha of the Burmese, Rangoon, 1866, p. 504. Cf. *G*âtaka, vol. ii. p. 198.

*Three reads on* Kâsâva or Kâshâya*: no comma after* Kâsâva *at 1.5× and 6×, a comma once at 3× — given without. The* g *of* Dharmadhva*g*ânâ*m* *is italic at 3× (the 1.5× read hedged "plain g"). Digits* LXIII, 36 · XII, 568 · 1866 · 504 · ii. p. 198 *each read at 1.5× and 3×.*

### Chinese (T210 法句經 雙要品第九, Taishō vol. 4 p. 562a26b–a27b [leaf 0561]) — identification ours
> `T04n0210_p0562a26` 〔如風大山 — end of the v. 8 stanza〕　不吐毒態　欲心馳騁

*On the parallel.* 不吐毒態 欲心馳騁 未能自調 不應法衣 — not having spat out the poisonous disposition, the desiring mind galloping, not yet able to tame himself: he is not fit for the Dharma-robe — answers v. 9 pāda by pāda (anikkasāvo / *paridahessati* is not rendered — the robe appears only in the last pāda / apeto damasaccena ≈ 未能自調 / na so kāsāvaṁ arhati ≈ 不應法衣), with 毒態 for *kasāva* and 法衣 for *kāsāva* — the Pāli pun on *kasāva/kāsāva* that Müller's note 9 discusses is not reproducible in the Chinese and is not attempted. > Asāre sāramatino sāre câsāradassino¹  
> te sāraṁ nâdhigacchanti micchāsaṁkappagocarā.

*Both circumflexes peaked (3×, 8×); no punctuation after* câsāradassino¹ *(8×).*

Fausbøll, *Variants and Notes*, V. 11 (foot of leaf 0021, line 5 — the last apparatus line of the leaf — running over onto the first apparatus line of leaf 0022): ¹ Cᵏ ca asāra-. *Saṁkappa* cfr. vv. 74. 147. 280. | 339. Cfr. Divyāvadāna p. 384,5.

*The siglum* Cᵏ *carries no digit (8×, 12×);* Saṁkappa *is letter-spaced (8×, 12×); every numeral of the list is followed by a period, not a comma (8×, 12×). The leaf break | falls between* 280. *and* 339. *— leaf 0021's foot ends with* 280. *and leaf 0022's foot opens* 339. Cfr. Divyāvadāna p. 384,5. *(2.5×, 10×), after which* V. 13. *follows on the same line: there is no* V. 12. *The 5 of* 384,5 *is a small figure on the baseline (10×, 16×), i.e. page 384, line 5.*

### English (Müller 1881, SBE X, p. 6; verse 11)
> 11. They who imagine truth in untruth, and see untruth in truth, never arrive at truth, but follow vain desires.

Müller, note 11, 12 (p. 6; one note for both verses, keyed `11, 12.`): 11, 12. Sâra, which I have translated by ' truth,' has many meanings in Sanskrit. It means the sap of a thing, then essence or reality ; in a metaphysical sense, the highest reality ; in a moral sense, truth. It is impossible in a translation to do more than indicate the meaning of such words, and in order to understand them fully, we must know not only their definition, but their history. See Beal, Dhammapada, p. 64.

No note numeral in v. 12 (checked at 3× and 6× on both lines), and **no `V. 12.` in the apparatus** — the foot of leaf 0022 passes from V. 11's run-over to `V. 13.` (2.5×, 10×). *Sārañ ca* and *asārañ ca* with the tilde, *adhigacchanti* without the circumflex of v. 11's *nâdhigacchanti* — both as printed.

### English (Müller 1881, SBE X, p. 6; verse 12)

*On the parallel.* 知眞爲眞 見僞知僞 是爲正計 必得眞利 — knowing the true as true, seeing the false and knowing it false: this is right reckoning, and one surely obtains the true benefit — answers v. 12 pāda by pāda (sārañ ca sārato ñatvā / asārañ ca asārato / sammāsaṁkappagocarā ≈ 是爲正計 / te sāraṁ adhigacchanti ≈ 必得眞利), the twin of the a29–b01a stanza as v. 12 is of v. 11. The Taishō's ⓰ records that the 聖語藏 witness omits exactly this stanza — the Chinese twin is therefore attested in the base text and in 宋元明 but not in 聖. The identification is Sarasvatī's; the Taishō prints none.
