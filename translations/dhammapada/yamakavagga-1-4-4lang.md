## Conventions

- **Fausbøll**: his anusvāra is **ṁ (dot above)** throughout (naṁ, cakkaṁ, padaṁ, maṁ, taṁ, veraṁ, tesaṁ, kudācanaṁ), never ṃ; the velar nasal in *Manopubbaṅgamā* is ṅ (mark above). His verse numbers are small old-style figures in the left margin (`1.` … `4.`); the initial M of v. 1 is a two-line drop capital. His quotation marks are the Continental pair „ … “. His note numerals are superscripts in the text, keyed to `V. 1.`, `V. 3.`, `V. 4` in the foot apparatus under the sub-heading *Variants and Notes.*; letter-spaced words in the apparatus (D h a m m a, A j i n i, T a ṁ) are given here in *italics*; the Danish sign `ɔ:` ("i.e.") is kept. His sigla, from his own Abbreviations list (pp. XI–XIII, read from the leaves): **Bʳ** = "Dhammapada printed in Burmese characters at Rangoon. Sakkarāj 1242."; **Cᶜ** = "The Dhammapada or Principles of Morality. With a Sinhalese Translation, revised by H. Devamitta. Colombo 1879."; **Cᵏ¹⁻³** = "The three different MSS. of the Dhammapada in cingalese characters kept at the Great Royal Library of Kopenhagen. See the Edition of Dhp. 1855."; **Sᵏ** = "The Edition of the Dhp. in the 25ᵗʰ vol. of the King of Siam's Edition of the Buddhist Canon, printed in Siamese characters."; **Ct.** = Commentary, **Ctator** = Commentator. Two accidental marks on the leaf are *not* reproduced: a stray sliver before *va* in v. 1c (lighter and thinner than the type, read at 10× as dirt or a risen space, not a hyphen or apostrophe), and a displaced dot over the *a* of *anveti* in v. 2c (the *m* of *sukham* itself carries no dot); both are recorded in the review section.
- **Müller**: his printer sets a thin space before `:` `;` `!` `?` — reproduced here as an ordinary space (`thought :`). His notes are **numbered by verse** (`1.`, `2.`, `3.` in the foot, full-size figures with a period) and carry **no reference numerals in the verse text** — checked at 2–3× on every line end of vv. 1–4 and on the verse numbers themselves. Line breaks and end-of-line hyphens of the verses are not kept (prose); those of the notes are not kept either, but every word, accent, quotation mark and italic is. Müller's SBE transliteration: â î û with circumflex for long vowels, **ṅ** (dot above) for the velar nasal (saṅkhâra, mano pubbaṅgamâ), and *italic* consonants for the palatals/retroflexes — *k* = c, *kh* = ch, *n* = ṇ, *s* = ś — e.g. akko*kkh*i, Ka*kk*âyana, viññâ*n*a, kru*s*. Italics in this column are Müller's (checked at 2.5×), not editorial.
- **Taishō**: the verses of T210 are set in four-character pādas separated by a one-character blank, **without 句點**; the only rings on these columns are in the prose preface (a11: 者。 對。). The Taishō prints **Japanese kaeriten (返り点: 一 二 レ)** in small type at the lower corners of characters throughout this text — they are recorded under each column as read, since they are part of what the edition prints, but they are reading aids of the 1924–34 editors, not text. Ringed numerals ❹–⓬ stand in the column exactly where printed; the foot apparatus (six horizontal lines of small type, read **left to right**) is given for every note that falls in a10–a22, with the sigla as printed — a ring containing 宋 / 元 / 明 / 聖, rendered here as 【宋】【元】【明】【聖】, and a **striped ring** (three bars), rendered 【三】, which the Taishō's convention expands as 宋元明 — that expansion is stated, not read, because this scan carries no 凡例 leaf. The modern punctuation of CBETA (，；。？) was **not** inherited; CBETA's TEI (`cbeta-org/xml-p5`, `T/T04/T04n0210.xml`) was used only to predict line numbers and to list the note numbers to look for, and one of its readings is contradicted by the leaf (note ❿, below). Glyph forms as printed: 爲 卽 擧 甞 妬 慍.

## Verse 1 · Manopubbaṅgamā … dukkham anveti

### Pāli (Fausbøll 1900, p. 2 [unnumbered leaf 0019]; verse 1)
> Manopubbaṅgamā dhammā manoseṭṭhā manomayā,  
> manasā ce paduṭṭhena bhāsatī¹ vā karoti vā  
> tato naṁ dukkham anveti cakkaṁ va vahato padaṁ.

Fausbøll, *Variants and Notes*, V. 1: ¹ MSS. & Editions bhāsati. *Dhamma*, cfr. v. 242, Jātaka II ¹⁹⁸⁄₁₄.

### English (Müller 1881, SBE X, p. 3 [unnumbered tif 0063]; verse 1)
> 1. All that we are is the result of what we have thought : it is founded on our thoughts, it is made up of our thoughts. If a man speaks or acts with an evil thought, pain follows him, as the wheel follows the foot of the ox that draws the carriage.

Müller, note 1 (pp. 3–4): 1. Dharma, though clear in its meaning, is difficult to translate. It has different meanings in different systems of philosophy, and its peculiar application in the phraseology of Buddhism has been fully elucidated by Burnouf, Introduction à l'Histoire du Buddhisme, p. 41 seq. He writes : ' Je traduis ordinairement ce terme par condition, d'autres fois par lois, mais aucune de ces traductions n'est parfaitement complète ; il faut entendre par dharma ce qui fait qu'une chose est ce qu'elle est, ce qui constitue sa nature propre, comme l'a bien montré Lassen, à l'occasion de la célèbre formule, " Ye dharmâ hetuprabhavâ." ' Etymologically the Latin for-ma expresses the same general idea which was expressed by dhar-ma. See also Burnouf, Lotus de la bonne Loi, p. 524. Fausböll translates : ' Naturae a mente principium ducunt,' which shows that he rightly understood dharma in the Buddhist sense. Gogerly (see Spence Hardy, Eastern Monachism, p. 28) translates : ' Mind precedes action,' which, if not wrong, is at all events wrongly expressed ; while Professor Weber's rendering, ' Die Pflichten aus dem Herz folgern,' is quite inadmissible. D'Alwis (Buddhist Nirwana, p. 70 seq.), following the commentary, proposes to give a more technical interpretation of this verse, viz. ' Mind is the leader of all its faculties. Mind is the chief (of all its faculties). The very mind is made up of those (faculties). If one speaks or acts with a polluted mind, then affliction follows him as the wheel follows the feet of the bearer (the bullock).' To me this technical acceptation seems not applicable here, where we have to deal with the simplest moral precepts, and not with psychological niceties of Buddhist philosophy. It should be stated, however, that Childers, who first (s. v. dhamma) approved of my translation, seems afterwards to have changed his opinion. On p. 120 of his excellent Pâli Dictionary he said : ' Three of the five khandhas, viz. vedanâ, saññâ, and saṅkhâra, are collectively termed dhammâ (plur.), " mental faculties," and in the first verse of Dhammapada the commentator takes the word dhammâ to mean those three faculties. But this interpretation appears forced and unnatural, and I look upon Dr. Max Müller's translation, " All that we are is the result of what we have thought," as the best possible rendering of the spirit of the phrase mano pubbaṅgamâ dhammâ.' But on p. 577 the same scholar writes : ' Of the four mental khandhas the superiority of viññâ*n*a is strongly asserted in the first verse of Dhammapada, " The mental faculties (vedanâ, saññâ, and saṅkhâra) are dominated by Mind, they are governed by Mind, they are made up of Mind." That this is the true meaning of the passage I am now convinced ; see D'Alwis, Nirwana, pp. 70–75.' I do not deny that this may have been the traditional interpretation, at all events since the days of Buddhaghosa, but the very legend quoted by Buddhaghosa in illustration of this verse shows that its simpler and purely moral interpretation was likewise supported by tradition, and I therefore adhere to my original translation.

## Verse 3 · Akkocchi maṁ … veraṁ tesaṁ na sammati

### Pāli (Fausbøll 1900, p. 2; verse 3)
> „Akkocchi maṁ avadhi maṁ ajini¹ maṁ ahāsi me“,  
> ye taṁ upanayihanti² veraṁ tesaṁ na sammati.

Fausbøll, *Variants and Notes*, V. 3: ¹ Bʳ Sᵏ ye ca. ² Cᵏ³ Bʳ Sᵏ upanayhanti, Cᵏ² Cᵏ¹ and the Commentary upanayihanti, cfr. Note in Dhp. 1855. *Ajini*, read ajinī? *Taṁ* ɔ: kodhaṁ, Ctary. Verses 3—6 are taken from Jāt. III p. 488, cfr. p. 212. Vinaya I p. 349.

*As printed, the numeral ¹ stands after* ajini *(read at 4× and 8×), while the note it keys reads* Bʳ Sᵏ ye ca *— a variant that belongs to the* ye taṁ *of the second line (compare V. 4 n. ¹). The placement is Fausbøll's or his printer's and is reproduced, not corrected; a reader should treat* ye ca *as the Bʳ Sᵏ reading for* ye taṁ.

### English (Müller 1881, SBE X, p. 4; verse 3)
> 3. ' He abused me, he beat me, he defeated me, he robbed me,'—in those who harbour such thoughts hatred will never cease.

Müller, note 3 (p. 4): 3. On akko*kkh*i, see Ka*kk*âyana VI, 4, 17. D'Alwis, Pâli Grammar, p. 38 note. ' When akko*kkh*i means " he abused," it is derived from kru*s*, not from krudh.' See Senart, Ka*kk*âyana, l. c.

### Chinese (T210, Taishō vol. 4 p. 562a19–a20) — see the note on the parallel
> `T04n0210_p0562a19` 不爲妬嫉　❾敏達善言　❿慍於怨者  
> `T04n0210_p0562a20` 未⓫甞無怨　不＊慍自除　是道可宗

Kaeriten as read: a19 不⁽レ⁾ 爲⁽二⁾ 嫉⁽一⁾ 敏⁽二⁾ 言⁽一⁾ 慍⁽二⁾ 者⁽一⁾; a20 未⁽二⁾ 無⁽レ⁾ (one read: 一レ) 不⁽レ⁾ 可⁽レ⁾. The ＊ after 不 in a20 is the Taishō's recurrence mark attached to note ❿. Taishō foot notes: ❾ 敏＝愍 【三】 【聖】; ❿ 慍＝慢 【聖】 ＊ — **the variant is read 慢 twice (3×, 6×; 忄 + 曰/罒/又, no 木 and no triple 口); CBETA prints 懆【聖】＊ — not inherited, flagged**; ⓫ 甞＝常 【聖】.

**On the parallel.** The Taishō prints no Pāli verse numbers beside T210's stanzas; the equivalence it *does* print is note ❹ (chapter = *1. Yamakavagga.*) and note ❽ on a17 (**下二頌巴利文無** — "the two stanzas below have no Pāli", i.e. 隨亂意行…何解善言 and 隨正意行…敏達善言, a17–a19b). What stands after those two in T210 is the stanza 慍於怨者 未甞無怨 不慍自除 是道可宗 (a19c–a20). Its sense — hatred is never absent in one who resents; when one does not resent it is removed of itself; this is the way to be honoured — covers Dhp 3, 4 **and the first half of 5** (na hi verena verāni sammanti … averena ca sammanti) in a single stanza. It is therefore given here as the T210 counterpart of vv. 3–4 *jointly*, and the identification is Sarasvatī's, not the Taishō's. The two intervening stanzas the Taishō marks as without Pāli are printed in the heading block below so the sequence can be checked.
