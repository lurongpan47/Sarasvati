## The Manu II.121 parallel — the chapter's cross-tradition set-piece on Fausbøll 0043 L5–L6 and Müller p. 33 note 109

Both editors carry the Dharmaśāstra parallel to Dhp 109 as the chapter's most elaborate text-critical event. The parallel is Manu-smṛti II.121, the four-blessings-to-the-elder-reverer line. The TWO EDITIONS carry DIFFERENT READINGS of the SAME Sanskrit source:

**Fausbøll 0043 L5–L6 (indented, in his own Sanskrit convention):**

> Abhivādanaçīlasya nityaṁ vṛddhopasevinah  
> catvāri tasya vṛddhante āyur vidyā yaço balaṁ.

*Fausbøll's Sanskrit uses:* **ç** (c with cedilla) for standard ś (`Abhivādanaç-` for standard `Abhivādanaś-`, `yaço` for standard `yaśo`); **ṛ** (r with underdot) for Vedic vocalic ṛ (`vṛddhopasevinah`, `vṛddhante`); **plain h** (no visarga ḥ dot) for standard ḥ (`vinah` for standard `vinaḥ`); **ṁ** (dotted anusvāra) for standard ṃ; **ā, ī, ū** macron. The verb reading is **`vṛddhante`** (3sg. middle present) with the pronoun **`tasya`** ("to him") — a shorter / earlier reading. Fausbøll's final `balaṁ.` ends on dotted anusvāra + period.

**Müller p. 33 note 109 (indented, in SBE X italic transliteration):**

> Abhivâdana*s*îlasya nitya*m* v*ri*ddhopasevina*h*,  
> *K*atvâri sampravardhante âyur vidyâ ya*s*o balam.

*Müller's Sanskrit uses:* **italic *s*** for ś; **italic *ri*** for ṛ (as a letter-pair, not an underdotted ṛ); **italic *h*** for ḥ (not plain h); **italic *m*** for ṃ; **italic *K*** for IAST ca (specifically for Devanāgari क as it appears in SBE's convention for conjunct ca/c); **â, î, û** circumflex (not macron). The verb reading is **`sampravardhante`** (3pl. middle present with sam-pra- prefix "they fully increase") with NO pronoun — the longer / standard Dharmaśāstra reading. Müller's final `balam.` ends on plain m + period (NOT dotted anusvāra).

**The text-critical disagreement**: the TWO EDITORS carry DIFFERENT MANU II.121 READINGS on both the verb and the pronoun. Fausbøll's `catvāri tasya vṛddhante` → Müller's `*K*atvâri sampravardhante`. Possible explanations:

- Fausbøll cites a shorter manuscript tradition of Manu-smṛti (possibly a Sinhalese or South Indian recension known to the Buddhist editors), Müller cites the standard Dharmaśāstra reading (the northern recension published by Jolly 1887 or by Haughton 1825).
- Both readings are attested in Manu-smṛti II.121 — the verse exists in multiple recensions, and the two editors chose independently.
- One editor's reading is a copyist's error from his own source.

**The transliteration disagreement**: beyond the text, the two editions use ENTIRELY DIFFERENT transliteration schemes for Sanskrit. Fausbøll uses a REDUCED diacritic set (ç, ṛ, plain h, ṁ, macron), omitting visarga and reducing ś to the French-origin ç — a convention closer to the 1860s–70s Danish Oriental philology. Müller uses the SBE X italic-letter scheme (italic consonants mark IAST non-roman sounds, circumflex for long vowels) — a convention standardised across the entire Sacred Books of the East series. The SIDE-BY-SIDE comparison on Dhp 109 is the SAMPLES' FIRST attestation of both schemes on the SAME source text.

Nominated #1 (the two-editor Manu II.121 disagreement). A Sanskritist + a Dharmaśāstra scholar + a historian of Victorian Oriental philology to open against Jolly's 1887 critical Manu edition, the Āpastamba-sūtras edition cited by Müller, and the Asiatic Researches XX p. 259 for the Siamese Buddhist liturgical use.
