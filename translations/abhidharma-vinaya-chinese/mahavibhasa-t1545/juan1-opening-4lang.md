# Abhidharma Mahāvibhāṣā, juan 1 opening · 阿毘達磨大毘婆沙論卷第一 · 開頭

*The compendium's opening page: the Taishō's own cross-reference line, the title + author + translator block, Hsüan-tsang's preface heading, and the start of the preface's great Q&A on who composed the Vibhāṣā — three attested lines of the Chinese column copied from a page image, with three Sarasvatī drafts below · Chinese (`chinese`) branch · `abhidharma-vinaya-chinese` sub-branch · Tier 1 Rank 3 · the **first scan-first sample ever in this sub-branch**, and the first in Sarasvatī of a Chinese-only abhidharma text · built scan-first 2026-11-02 15:00 PST*

## Why Tier 1

T1545 阿毘達磨大毘婆沙論 (Abhidharma-mahāvibhāṣā-śāstra, "The Great Exposition") is **the flagship orphan of the Sarvāstivāda canon in Chinese**. In 200 fascicles (juan), it is Hsüan-tsang's (玄奘, Xuánzàng) 7th-century translation of the exhaustive commentary of the Kashmirian Sarvāstivāda school on the Jñānaprasthāna (T1544 阿毘達磨發智論, Kātyāyanīputra). Its Sanskrit original is **lost**. No Tibetan translation was ever made (the Tibetan canon's Abhidharma literature follows the Yogācāra and Theravāda-adjacent streams; the Sarvāstivāda Vibhāṣā tradition did not reach Tibet via the Kangyur / Tenjur). The sole complete surviving witness is this Chinese translation, with a prior partial version in Buddhavarman's T1546 阿毘曇毘婆沙論 (60 juan, incomplete, 437–439 CE).

**As of 2026 there exists no complete modern translation of T1545 in any language.** Scholarly partial renderings exist — the Encyclopedia of Indian Philosophies vol. VIII (Potter, Buswell, Jaini et al. 1999) summarises large portions; KL Dhammajoti's *Sarvāstivāda Abhidharma* (2007) translates many passages; Nishi, Fukuhara, Katō and other Japanese scholars have translated extracts; a small Chinese academic 白話 ("plain language") committee has published juan 1 only (馮承鈞 1930s, 吕澂 fragments 1950s, 法鼓 selections 2010s). None of these is comprehensive. The 200-juan whole has never been rendered as a modern reader's edition.

This is Tier 1 Rank 3 (per ROADMAP § Orphan Tier, 2026-11-02): fully electronic (CBETA), image-digitised (IA `taisho-tripitaka`), and yet effectively unreadable to anyone who does not read 7th-century Chinese Buddhist translationese and know Sarvāstivāda technical vocabulary. The first-sample demand is therefore not for text acquisition — it is for a **scan-first, draft-marked, reviewer-open framework** that lets T1545 be opened one juan at a time, four columns wide, over however many years the project runs. This is sample 1 of 200.

## Conventions

- **Taishō (large daizōkyō conventions)**: traditional 3-register page (a / b / c, top to bottom), each register with ~25 columns of 17 characters, read right-to-left; Taishō number headers in modern Western type (`No. 1545 (No. 1546, cf. Nos. 1543, 1544, 1547)`) printed horizontally at the top-right of the frame; author / translator / preface heading indented a few positions below the top rule; ringed footnote numerals ❶❷❸… keyed to a foot apparatus line printed in small type below register c; `。` as the small ○ ring in position (modern Taishō convention); sigla as circled characters (㊀=宋, ㊁=元, ㊂=明, ㊄=宮, ㊅=聖, ＊=general reference; `㊂` with three horizontal strokes = 三 = 宋元明 combined, a common Taishō shorthand); kaeriten / okurigana are the 1924–34 editors' Japanese reading aids where they appear (**no kaeriten appear on this leaf**, as the preface is prose Chinese not read with Japanese kundoku here).
- **Honorific indentation**: traditional Chinese convention of inserting a one-character blank before 詔 (imperial decree), 聖 (sage), 佛 (Buddha), etc. Preserved as `⌴` where present on the leaf. Here: `❶三藏法師玄奘奉⌴詔譯` — the one-position blank before 詔譯 is a visible honorific gap. (CBETA's digital text drops honorific blanks silently; the leaf prints them.)
- **Glyph forms as printed**: **毘** (田 + 比, not 毗 or 毘'); **磨** (not 摩); **沙** (氵 + 少); **達** (辶 + 大 + 羊 top); **羅** (罒 + 糸 + 隹); **藏** (艹 + 臧); **玄奘** (standard); **苾** (艹 + 必); **芻** (勹 + 卯 style); **髮** (髟 + 犮); **僧伽胝** (saṁghāṭi, outer robe); **苾芻** (bhikṣu, standard Hsüan-tsang transliteration — the leaf prints 苾芻 not 比丘). ## Preface body, first Q&A · 問誰造此論。答佛世尊。…

### 汉文 (T1545, Taishō vol. 27 p. 1a, columns c05–c20 — the first great Q&A of the preface)

> `T27n1545_p0001a05` 問誰造此論。答佛世尊。所以者何。以一切  
> `T27n1545_p0001a06` 種所知法性甚深微妙。非佛世尊一切智者。  
> `T27n1545_p0001a07` 誰能究竟等覺開示。若爾此中誰問誰答。或  
> `T27n1545_p0001a08` 有說者。尊者舍利子問。佛世尊答。復有說  
> `T27n1545_p0001a09` 者。五百阿羅漢問。佛世尊答。有作是說。諸  
> `T27n1545_p0001a10` 天神問。佛世尊答。有餘師說。化苾芻問。佛世  
> `T27n1545_p0001a11` 尊答。所以者何。諸佛法爾所知法性。於諸世  
> `T27n1545_p0001a12` 間定應開示。然無問者。爾時世尊化作苾  
> `T27n1545_p0001a13` 芻。形容端正衆所樂見。剃除鬚髮服僧伽  
> `T27n1545_p0001a14` 胝。令彼請問佛世尊答。猶如徵問義品因  
> `T27n1545_p0001a15` 緣。問若爾此論何故傳言。尊者迦多衍尼子  
> `T27n1545_p0001a16` 造。答由彼尊者受持演說廣令流布。是故此  
> `T27n1545_p0001a17` 論名稱歸彼。然是佛說。復有說者。此論即  
> `T27n1545_p0001a18` 彼尊者迦多衍尼子造。問豈不前言以一切  
> `T27n1545_p0001a19` 種所知法性甚深微妙。非佛世尊一切智者  
> `T27n1545_p0001a20` 誰能究竟等覺開示。云何彼尊者能造此論

*Read at 2× (whole register a) and 3× (c01–c08). Each column holds 17 characters of normal body type, running top to bottom; column breaks here mirror the leaf's column breaks exactly. **No kaeriten, no ringed numerals and no 。 line-breaks interrupt the body text of these 16 columns.** All 。 full-stops are the small ○ ring. CBETA agrees letter-for-letter at this zoom; one glyph flagged — **苾芻** (c10, c13) is the leaf's reading (standard Hsüan-tsang transliteration of bhikṣu, "mendicant"); CBETA normalises identically. **耶 at the end of c20 is cut by the right edge of the next column's reading-boundary and continues onto c21** (confirmed at 2×).*

*This is the opening of a chain-of-answers Q&A that is one of the Mahāvibhāṣā's most celebrated passages:*

- **Q1**: Who composed this treatise?
- **A1**: The Buddha, the Bhagavān.
- **Q2 (implicit)**: Why?
- **A2**: Because the nature of all phenomena, in all their species, is profound and subtle; no one but a perfectly awakened Bhagavān, an all-knowing one, can fully awaken to it and reveal it.
- **Q3**: Then who in this [compendium] is the questioner and who the answerer?
- **A3** (one view): The Elder Śāriputra asks; the Buddha the Bhagavān answers.
- **A3** (another view): The five hundred arhats ask; the Buddha the Bhagavān answers.
- **A3** (another view): The devas ask; the Buddha the Bhagavān answers.
- **A3** (a further master): A conjured monk (化苾芻) asks; the Buddha the Bhagavān answers. [Why?] Because the Buddhas' innate nature of knowing all things must be revealed to all worlds; but if there is no one asking, the Bhagavān at that time conjures a monk of handsome form, appealing to all beings, having shaved his head and donned the saṁghāṭi robe, and has that monk ask so that the Bhagavān may answer — as in the questioner of the *Arthapada-vagga* (c13–c15).
- **Q4**: If so, why is this treatise traditionally said to be composed by the Elder Kātyāyanīputra?
- **A4**: Because that Elder received, preserved, expounded and widely propagated it; it is on that account named after him. But it is **the Buddha's own teaching**.
- **A4 (variant)**: Some say this treatise is in fact composed by that Elder Kātyāyanīputra himself.
- **Q5**: But if (as said above) the nature of all phenomena is so profound that no one but the Bhagavān can perfectly reveal it, how could that Elder compose this treatise?

*The chain goes on through many columns of register a and into register b. This sample stops at Q5 (the end of c20) as the natural pause before the long resolution that begins c21: "**答以彼尊者亦有微妙甚深猛利善巧覺慧…**" ("Answer: because that Elder too possesses subtle, profound, keen, skilful awakening-wisdom…"). The question-and-answer form is the whole structural device of the Vibhāṣā — this opening passage is where the device itself is justified.*

### 现代汉语 (Sarasvatī machine draft ⟨བརྟག⟩ — awaiting review)

> **问：** 这部论是谁所造？
> **答：** 是佛世尊所造。
> **问：** 为什么这样说？
> **答：** 因为一切种类的所知法的本性极为深奥微妙，若非佛陀世尊这样的一切智者，谁能彻底证悟并开示？
> **问：** 若是这样，那么本论中谁是问者，谁是答者？
> **答：** 一说：尊者舍利子问，佛世尊答。又一说：五百位阿罗汉问，佛世尊答。又有人说：诸天神问，佛世尊答。另有师说：是佛所化现的比丘问，佛世尊答。为什么？因为诸佛本性对一切所知法，于诸世间必应开示；可当时没有人问，于是世尊便化作一位比丘，容貌端正、众所乐见，剃除须发、身着僧伽胝衣，令此化人请问而佛世尊回答——就像《义品因缘》中那位问者一样。
> **问：** 若是这样，这部论为何相传说是由尊者迦多衍尼子所造？
> **答：** 因为是那位尊者受持、讲说并广为流布本论，所以本论的名声归属于他。但本论实际上是佛所说。另有人说：本论就是那位尊者迦多衍尼子所造。
> **问：** 前面不是说一切种所知法本性极为深奥微妙，非佛世尊这样的一切智者谁能彻底证悟并开示吗？那位尊者又如何能造此论呢？⟨བརྟག⟩

### English (Sarasvatī machine draft ⟨བརྟག⟩ — awaiting review)

> **Question:** Who composed this treatise?
> **Answer:** The Buddha, the Bhagavān.
> **Question:** How so?
> **Answer:** Because the nature of all cognisable phenomena, in all their species, is profoundly subtle; who but a Bhagavān, a fully awakened one, an all-knower, could fully awaken to it and disclose it?
> **Question:** If so, then in this [treatise] who is the questioner and who the answerer?
> **Answer:** Some say: the Elder Śāriputra asks; the Buddha the Bhagavān answers. Others say: the five hundred Arhats ask; the Buddha the Bhagavān answers. Some say: the devas ask; the Buddha the Bhagavān answers. And yet another master says: a magically-created monk (化苾芻 *nirmita-bhikṣu*) asks; the Buddha the Bhagavān answers. Why so? Because the Buddhas' very nature is to make known the nature of cognisable things to all worlds; but as there was no questioner, the Bhagavān at that time created a monk of handsome form, pleasing to all, with shaven head and the *saṁghāṭi* robe, and had that one ask so that the Bhagavān might answer — as in the questioner of the *Aṭṭhakavagga/Arthapada's* causal passages (c13–c15).
> **Question:** If so, why does the tradition say that this treatise was composed by the Elder Kātyāyanīputra?
> **Answer:** Because that Elder received, retained, expounded, and widely propagated it; and therefore the treatise's renown is attributed to him. Yet it is **the Buddha's own teaching**. Some say, however, that this treatise is in fact composed by that Elder Kātyāyanīputra himself.
> **Question:** But was it not said earlier that the nature of all cognisable phenomena, in all their species, is so profoundly subtle that none but a Buddha, the Bhagavān, an all-knower, could fully awaken to it and disclose it? How then could that Elder compose this treatise? ⟨བརྟག⟩

## Layout notes for future T1545 samples (first-sample learnings)

This is sample 1 of 200. Observations here are intended to save weeks of re-discovery across the next 199 samples:

1. **The task's expected opening-text prediction was wrong.** The brief predicted that p. 1a would open with `阿毘達磨大毘婆沙論卷第一 五百大阿羅漢等造 三藏法師玄奘奉詔譯 雜蘊第一中世第一納息第一之一 若世第一法與苦法智忍為等無間者…`. In fact, the leaf opens with the title block and then jumps **directly to the preface** (阿毘達磨發智大毘婆沙論序), not to the body-proper. The 雜蘊第一… body opening is on a **later leaf**. This means every juan of T1545 may have a juan-opening preface or 慧立/慧琳-style structural matter in registers a–c of the leaf that bears the juan heading. **Future T1545 samples**: when sampling the "opening" of a juan, always check whether the juan heading on the first leaf is immediately followed by body text or by a preface / structural layer.

2. **Register-counting**: register a has 25+ columns of 17 characters each. The task's "a26" is roughly the last column of register a — a reasonable sampling boundary for ONE register's worth of content. **Future samples**: a "one-register sample" is ≈ 25 × 17 = 425 characters of attested text; a "one-leaf sample" is ≈ 3 × 425 = 1275 characters; the full 200-juan compendium is ≈ 200 × 10 leaves × 1275 = 2,550,000 characters, roughly 4 MB of plain text. At this file's density (~16 KB per 400 characters attested), full T1545 coverage as 4-column scan-first samples would run to ≈ 100 MB of markdown. **This is a decade+ project at one-sample-per-day.**

3. **Taishō vol. 27 has a single-work layout**: the IA scan `taisho-tripitaka` → `大正高清調整版第027冊共1004頁_jp2.zip` holds ONLY T1545 across its 1004 pages (= ~1004 leaves ≈ 1000 of body text, 1 of title, 2–3 of back matter). **Future samples**: leaf = page = Taishō page number minus 1 (if leaf 0000 is p. 1). Confirm this leaf = page offset holds across the volume on sample 2.

4. **Foot apparatus density**: p. 1a has only 5 ringed entries. Later juan (e.g. 雜蘊 juan containing complex vinaya terminology) will have 15–30+ per page. The apparatus foot line extends from x ≈ 300 to x ≈ 3900 in a single row below register c; expect to need 3× zoom in both left and right halves.

5. **Honorific blanks** before 詔, 聖, 佛, 皇 — these are a traditional-print convention preserved by the Taishō. CBETA silently normalises them away. Sarasvatī files **should preserve** them as `⌴` to retain the typographic datum of the Taishō edition.

6. **Glyph forms**: the Taishō prints **毘** (not 毗), 磨 (not 摩), 苾芻 (not 比丘 — Hsüan-tsang's deliberate standardised transliteration), 僧伽胝 (saṁghāṭi), 迦多衍尼子 (Kātyāyanīputra; not 迦旃延子, the earlier Chinese variant). These are Hsüan-tsang's own standardised transliteration choices and should be preserved verbatim.

7. **Taishō cross-reference headers** (`No. 1545 (No. 1546, cf. Nos. 1543, 1544, 1547)`): these are the Taishō editors' modern cross-reference lines in Western type, printed horizontally at the top-right. They are NOT part of the Chinese translator-work; they are 1924-era editorial additions. Sarasvatī files should carry them because they document the editorial relationship (T1546 = Buddhavarman's partial earlier Vibhāṣā, T1543 ≈ T1544 Jñānaprasthāna, T1547 = Saṅghabhūti's partial Vibhāṣā), but should mark them explicitly as *Taishō cross-reference line*, not as part of the attested translation.

8. **No kaeriten on T1545**: unlike Dhammapada T210 which carries the 1924–34 editors' kundoku reading marks (一 二 レ 上 中 下), the Mahāvibhāṣā leaves carry **no kaeriten** — the preface's prose Chinese is not meant to be read with Japanese kundoku markup here. **Future samples**: expect zero kaeriten throughout T1545. (This matches the general Taishō convention: kaeriten appear mainly on verse texts and short sūtras, not on long prose śāstras.)

9. **Fetch pattern**: IA URL template `https://archive.org/download/taisho-tripitaka/{VOL_ZIP_NAME}/{VOL_ZIP_NAME}/{VOL_PNG_NAME}_{LEAF:04d}.jp2` with URL encoding. First fetch of vol 27 was first-try HTTP 200. **Future samples**: expect this pattern to hold throughout the taisho-tripitaka collection (confirmed for vols 4 (T210), 17 (T784), now 27 (T1545)).

10. **Chinese column fidelity gate**: for a Chinese-only orphan canon text, the attested column IS the text. There is no Sanskrit, Tibetan, Pāli, or Khotanese parallel to cross-check against (unlike Dhammapada). This raises the fidelity bar: **every character typo in the attested column is uncorrectable from parallel.** Hence the 2×/3×/4×/5× zoom protocol on every column, not just difficult ones. **Future samples**: budget more image calls per column than for Dhammapada (where parallels catch errors); plan 15–20 image calls per sample vs. Dhammapada's 7–15.
