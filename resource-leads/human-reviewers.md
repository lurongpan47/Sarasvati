## Cross-cutting note on outreach etiquette

Every one of these people gets hit constantly by "please review my thing" cold emails. To have a real chance, Pan's first message should:
1. Attach the specific ~1-page slice (not the whole charter or whole sutta table) with clear diff markers of what's uncertain.
2. Name the specific critical edition Sarasvatī is aligned to (PTS, BHS Nāgārī, Degé, Bibliotheca Polyglotta, etc.).
3. Offer either co-credit in the charter's `credits/` block or an acknowledgement they can list on their own CV — many of these scholars publish through Open Access channels and value formal citation more than money.
4. State up front that Sarasvatī is a CC-BY / free-cultural-works project (à la SuttaCentral / 84000 / Bibliotheca Polyglotta) — this filters for the community that already believes in the mission.

## Cross-language institutional partnerships (better than individual outreach)

For several branches, the *right* first move is not to email an individual professor but to open a dialogue with a project or institution that already does what Sarasvatī needs:

1. **84000 (Translating the Words of the Buddha)** — https://84000.co/about/team — for Tibetan canonical review pipeline. Editorial contact: editorial@84000.co. They have a proven review workflow with 250+ vetted translators across Kangyur languages.
2. **SuttaCentral** — https://suttacentral.net — for Pāli + parallel-Āgama alignment. Reachable via https://discourse.suttacentral.net. Ven. Sujato + Ven. Brahmali are active.
3. **BDRC (Buddhist Digital Resource Center)** — https://bdrc.io — for Tibetan, Khmer, Mongolian, Burmese manuscript verification. Their Khmer + Mongolian rare-books teams have exactly the philological expertise.
4. **Bibliotheca Polyglotta (University of Oslo)** — https://www2.hf.uio.no/polyglotta/ — for multi-language sūtra alignment. Prof. Jens Braarvig (Oslo) is the founder; if Sarasvatī's UX is genuinely similar to Bibliotheca Polyglotta's, Braarvig is the single most natural editorial ally.
5. **Digital Dictionary of Buddhism (DDB) + CJKV-E** — http://www.buddhism-dict.net — for East Asian Buddhist terminology (Muller).
6. **EFEO Khmer Manuscripts / FEMC** — https://khmer-manuscripts.bdrc.io — for Khmer.
7. **Fragile Palm Leaves Foundation** (Bangkok, Peter Skilling) — for Southeast Asian Pāli manuscripts.

Approaching these as *partnerships* (offering to open-source-license Sarasvatī's alignments for their catalogs) is likely to yield better long-term outcomes than one-by-one cold outreach.
