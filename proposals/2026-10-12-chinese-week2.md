## Candidates

### A. **四十二章經 T784, title block + the Han-Mingdi preface + sections 1–2** *(preferred — built this session)*
- **What**: the Taishō's main text of the *Sūtra in Forty-two Sections* (高麗藏 base): `No. 784`, the title 四十二章經 with its two notes, the translator line 後漢西域沙門迦葉摩騰共法蘭譯, the 161-character preface on Emperor Ming's dream and the embassy to the Yuezhi (which the 明 edition drops, n. ⑧), then 佛言。辭親出家爲道… (section 1, through 譬如四支斷不復用之) and 佛言。除鬚髮爲沙門… (section 2). **T17n0784 p. 722a10–b05**, one leaf.
- **Chinese**: *Taishō shinshū daizōkyō* vol. 17, no. 784, p. 722 — IA `taisho-tripitaka`, file `大正高清調整版第017冊共964頁`, **leaf 0721 = p. 722** (margin read: 七八四 四十二章經 / 七二二), full-size JP2 4000 × 5755 via the BookReader endpoint; registers located by projection; 17-character columns; the six-line foot apparatus ①–⑯ read in thirds at 2×. **Finding**: the apparatus of p. 722 ends mid-note (⑯ `…名之`) at the leaf's double rule, and neither p. 721's nor p. 723's foot carries the run-over — notes ⑰–㊷ of this page are not printed on any leaf opened this session.
- **English (PD)**: Samuel Beal, *A Catena of Buddhist Scriptures from the Chinese*, Trübner, London 1871, **pp. 190–192** — the preamble and §§ 1–4 (Beal translates the 明 / 守遂 recension, so his preamble = the Taishō's note ⑩, his § 1's "understands his own heart…" = note ⑫, his § 3 = note ⑯: the English column maps onto the main text *and* the apparatus, and the file says which is which). IA `buddhistscriptur00bealuoft` leaves 210–212 (= pp. 190–192, page numbers read from the leaves; leaf = djvu index + 1).
- **Targets**: 现代汉语 + བོད་ཡིག drafts ⟨བརྟག⟩. There is no Tibetan 四十二章經 in the Kangyur (a Manchu–Mongol–Tibetan–Chinese quadrilingual Qianlong edition exists but is not a canonical Tibetan transmission and is not on any open scan found).

## Public-domain evidence
The sūtra's Chinese text is pre-5th-century at the latest (the 高麗藏 recension is the one the Taishō prints). The Taishō *edition* (1924–34; Takakusu †1945, Watanabe †1933) — its punctuation, sigla and apparatus — is treated exactly as in the T389 file (public domain in Japan since 2016 under the 70-year rule; the text itself PD everywhere). Beal (1825–1889), London 1871 → PD worldwide.
