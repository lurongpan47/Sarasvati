# Silk Road (`silkroad`) · Weekly candidate proposal · 2026-09-14

**Branch selected**: Silk Road / Central Asia — the only *manuscript-language* Central Asian strand still without a Khotanese or Tocharian sample. Existing v0.9 asset is Gāndhārī only (Barua–Mitra 1921 edition of the Khotan Dharmapada). This proposal extends Silk Road coverage into **Khotanese** and **Tocharian** using editions safely in the public domain worldwide.

## Candidates (pick one; all three are drop-in for a 4-language reading page)

### A. **Khotanese Vajracchedikā** *(preferred)*
- Source: Sten Konow (1916), *The Vajracchedikā in the Old Khotanese Version of Eastern Turkestan*, in A. F. R. Hoernle, *Manuscript Remains of Buddhist Literature Found in Eastern Turkestan*, Oxford. Konow d. 1948 → PD worldwide (life+70 since 2019; US pre-1929 rule already covers it).
- Text: opening + closing chapters of the *Diamond Sūtra* in Old Khotanese, ~40 short verses, with Konow's English facing translation.
- Reading page: **Old Khotanese (Konow transcription) / English (Konow 1916) / 漢文 Kumārajīva T235 / Tibetan Derge Kangyur / modern English** — 5-column.
- Why: closes the Iranian-language gap in the archive; Vajracchedikā is already anchored in three other branches so parallels are obvious.

### B. **Tocharian B *Udānavarga* fragments**
- Source: Sieg & Siegling (1921), *Tocharische Sprachreste I*, Berlin — PD worldwide.
- Text: a dozen verses from Berlin Turfan collection, all with published Sanskrit parallels (Bernhard 1965 for the Sanskrit is not PD, so use Rockhill 1892 translation instead).
- Reading page: **Tocharian B / Sanskrit Udānavarga (Rockhill 1892 base) / English / 漢文 T210 Dhp / Pāli Dhp parallel**.

### C. **Khotanese *Book of Zambasta* chapter 5** *(the ethics chapter)*
- Source: Leumann (1933–36), *Das nordarische (sakische) Lehrgedicht des Buddhismus* — Leumann d. 1931, PD worldwide.
- ~50 verses on the pāramitās; original Khotanese composition, not translation. Fills the "indigenous Central Asian Buddhist voice" gap.
- Emmerick 1968 (still in copyright until 2071) intentionally **not** used; would require Leumann-only base.

## Public-domain evidence
All three base editions are pre-1929 (US) and their editors died before 1955 (life+70 elsewhere). Konow †1948, Sieg †1951, Siegling †1946, Leumann †1931. Zero copyright risk.
