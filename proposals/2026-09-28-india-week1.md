# India (`india`) · Weekly candidate proposal · 2026-09-28

**Branch selected**: `india` — the last of the seven non-Tibetan branches to receive a weekly proposal (Pāli 08-29 · Chinese 08-29 · Sanskrit 08-31 · Sinosphere 09-07 · Silk Road 09-14 · SEAsia 09-21). This week's candidates stay with the same public-domain edition — one scan already located, page images opened in this session — but move from *royal policy* to the earliest epigraphic evidence for *Buddhist texts as texts*.

## Candidates

### A. **Calcutta-Bairāṭ rock-inscription** (the "Bhābrū edict") *(preferred)*
- **What**: Aśoka's letter to the Magadha Saṅgha. 8 lines, 7 sentences (A–G). The king names **seven dhaṁma-paliyāyāni** — *Vinaya-samukase, Aliya-vasāṇi, Anāgata-bhayāni, Muni-gāthā, Moneya-sūte, Upatisa-pasine, Lāghulovāde* — and asks monks, nuns, laymen and laywomen to hear and reflect on them. The oldest dated reference to named Buddhist texts anywhere (3rd c. BCE); the hinge between the India branch and the Pāli branch.
- **Prakrit**: Hultzsch, *Inscriptions of Asoka* (CII I), Oxford 1925, text **pp. 172–173** (Roman rows, lines 1–8, notes 6–11 and 1–11); estampage plate facing p. 173 ("Scale One-Fourth", 8 lines).
- **English (PD)**: Hultzsch **pp. 173–174**, notes 12–19 and p. 174 n. 1 (the identifications of the seven texts: Oldenberg, Lévi, Neumann, Rhys Davids, Bloch, Edmunds, Kosambi).
- **Scan**: IA `InscriptionsOfAsoka.NewEditionByE.Hultzsch`, leaves **n349 = p. 172, n351 = plate, n352 = p. 173, n353 = p. 174** (leaf ≠ page − 136 past p. 143 because of interleaved plates; margins read, not inferred). Full-size JP2 via BookReader, 5909 × 8117.
- **Targets**: 现代汉语 + བོད་ཡིག drafts ⟨བརྟག⟩. No Chinese or Tibetan rendering of this inscription is known to us.

### B. **Rummindeī (Lumbinī) pillar-inscription**
- Hultzsch pp. 164–165 (leaves n338–n340 located). 5 lines: *hida Budhe jāte Sakyamunī ti*. Shortest possible sample; the Buddha's birthplace attested in stone. Natural second item if A goes quickly.

### C. **Sārnāth pillar-edict** (schism edict)
- Hultzsch pp. 161–164 (leaf n337 = p. 163). The Saṅgha-splitting prohibition; longer, more lacunae; parallel to Kauśāmbī and Sāñcī versions. Heavier apparatus; listed for completeness.

## Public-domain evidence
Hultzsch (1857–1927), Clarendon Press 1925 → pre-1929, PD in the US; author †1927 → PD life+70 worldwide since 1998. Same edition and IA item already used for REXII/REXIII, whose licence path is recorded in those files. The stone itself is 3rd c. BCE.
