## Candidates (3)

### 1. **Preferred — Wŏnhyo, *Ijang ŭi* 二障義 (Doctrine of the Two Hindrances)**
- **Author / date:** Wŏnhyo (元曉, 617–686), Silla Korea. Wonhyo's mid-period Yogācāra treatise reconciling kleśa-āvaraṇa / jñeya-āvaraṇa across Xuanzang's "new" and Paramārtha's "old" translation streams.
- **Length:** ~1 fascicle, ≈ 25–30k Hanmun characters.
- **Public-domain evidence:** author died 686 CE; Chinese text preserved in Taishō vol. 30/45 & Han'guk pulgyo chŏnsŏ (韓國佛教全書) HPC vol. 1 — text itself PD by ~2,000 years.
- **Target languages missing:** **Modern Chinese vernacular** (only classical Hanmun exists), **Tibetan** (never rendered), **Spanish/Portuguese** (no vernacular version). English exists as academic partial only — no clean CC edition.
- **Why it matters:** the Two Hindrances framework is the single most technically precise Yogācāra schema in Korean Buddhism; foundational for anyone tracing how Silla mediated Xuanzang's Yogācāra to Nara Japan.

### 2. Alt — Kūkai, *Hizō Hōyaku* 秘藏寶鑰 (Precious Key to the Secret Treasury)
- Kūkai (774–835), Heian Japan. 3-fascicle condensation of *Jūjūshinron*. Q&A format, ~40k characters. Yoshito Hakeda's 1972 English abridgement is copyrighted; full text has no free CC translation. Chinese/Korean/Vietnamese vernacular versions are gap targets.

### 3. Alt — *Balsim suhaeng jang* 發心修行章 (Awaken Your Mind & Practice)
- Wŏnhyo, ~700 characters, exhortation verse. English exists in CWKB (Muller). Attractive as a *charter-style short text* for 12-language parallel reading — a natural companion to DN 16.
