# Weekly Branch Proposal · Sanskrit 梵文写本系

**Date:** 2026-08-31
**Branch:** `sanskrit`
**Source pool:** GRETIL (Göttingen Register of Electronic Texts in Indian Languages), SUB Göttingen.

## Candidates

1. **Vajracchedikā Prajñāpāramitā Sūtra (Gilgit recension)** — *金剛經 (Gilgit 本)*
   - ~4,000 Sanskrit words. GRETIL machine-readable edition based on Conze (1957) + Gilgit ms. facsimile (Vira & Chandra, 1959–74 — Śata-piṭaka series, PD in origin countries).
   - Target languages: modern Tibetan colloquial + literary Vietnamese (both currently lack a Sanskrit-direct, non-Chinese-mediated modern rendering).

2. **Prajñāpāramitāhṛdaya (Heart Sutra), short + long recensions** — *般若心經*
   - ~250 / ~600 Sanskrit words. Multiple critical editions on GRETIL (Conze 1948, Vaidya 1961); Vaidya is 60+ years old, PD in most jurisdictions; text itself pre-8th c.
   - Target languages: side-by-side Sanskrit → Pāli-idiom English + Khmer (bridge into `seasia` next quarter).

3. **Bhadracarīpraṇidhāna (Vows of Good Conduct)** — *普賢行願讚*
   - ~1,700 Sanskrit words, verse. GRETIL text from Suzuki (1961 reprint of Watanabe 1912 ed. — PD). Never rendered as a standalone cross-lingual reading with Sanskrit–Chinese–Tibetan–English aligned.
   - Target languages: aligned reading in the four languages, matching the DN 16 template.

## Why this branch, this week

- v0.9.0 milestone explicitly names Sanskrit as the third cross-branch sample.
- Vajracchedikā is *the* text where Sanskrit ↔ Chinese ↔ Tibetan divergence is scholarly rich; a public-domain aligned reading is a genuine gap.
- Bhadracarī gives us a short verse text that pairs cleanly with the DN 16 four-language template — same shape, different tradition.

## Public domain evidence

- GRETIL corpus: encoded editions redistributable under the GRETIL terms; underlying editions (Conze 1948/1957, Vaidya 1961, Watanabe 1912, Suzuki 1961 reprint) all published >60 years ago or by institutions releasing them for scholarly reuse.
- Gilgit manuscript facsimiles: originals 6th–7th c.; facsimile edition (Vira/Chandra) PD in India (>60 yr rule for institutional publications after author death or unclaimed).
- We will document each edition's PD basis inline in the translation header, per Foundational Principle #1.

- Tokens (draft + alignment + 4-language commentary): ~35k in / ~50k out (Opus tier).
- Human review: ~4h Sanskritist + ~2h Tibetan reviewer + ~1h Sinologist for aligned Chinese column.
